## Physical Description

A desert-adapted variant of the Rune Belt Woh, the Desert Woh is a powerful, broad-shouldered mount built for endurance across the endless sands. Its hide is tough and leathery, ranging from pale gold to deep ochre, providing natural camouflage against the desert backdrop. Its legs are shorter and stockier than its Rune Belt cousin, with broad, padded feet that distribute weight across the shifting sands. A thick, muscular hump rises behind its shoulders, storing water and fat for long journeys.

## Habitat and Range

Native to the eastern reaches of the Steadfast Desert, Desert Woh are found primarily in the territory controlled by the Bright Paw Wengari. They are rarely seen outside the desert—the climate of the Rune Belt is too moist for their constitution.

## Behavior

Desert Woh are patient, stubborn creatures with a strong herd instinct. They are not as aggressive as Rune Belt Woh, but they are far more enduring. A trained Desert Woh can carry a rider and full battle gear for three days without water, drawing on the reserves stored in its hump. In battle, they are steady and fearless, holding formation even under withering fire.

## Relationship to Wengari

The Bright Paw Wengari have bred Desert Woh for centuries as their primary heavy cavalry mount. Unlike the faster Pearly Scorpions favored by the Hummans, Desert Woh are chosen for reliability and endurance—they do not panic, they do not bolt, and they will stand their ground against anything short of a Tide Wolf pack.

During the Great War, a contingent of Bright Paw Desert Woh riders arrived at the siege of Styxian in the critical hour, their mounts still fresh after a forced march across the open desert. The riders were the eastern border garrison, and their arrival turned the tide of the battle.

## Combat Role

Desert Woh riders form the heavy cavalry backbone of Bright Paw military strategy. A full charge of Desert Woh riders is a devastating spectacle—the thundering of their padded feet, the glint of lacquered claws above their broad backs, the golden manes of their Bright Paw riders streaming in the wind.

## Lifecycle

Desert Woh foals are born during the cooler months and reach full size in three years. They bond with their riders through a shared-warmth ritual, where the rider sleeps beside the Woh through a desert night—a test of trust and endurance that forges a lifelong bond.
