## Chapter 6: 08:20 — The Light Shield Falls

It was 8:20 in the morning, the seventh day of the Month of Storms, in the first year of the reign of Ajani Brightmane. The light shield was gone. The wall was breaking. The reinforcements were still out in the desert, riding as fast as they could. And the great war was about to be lost.

M'rak saw the walls of the capital and felt hope for the first time in hours.

He had been riding since four in the morning, his Woh thundering across the desert at full pace, the eastern garrison's thirty riders stretched out behind him in a long, golden column. The sun was well above the horizon now, and the heat was beginning to build, and his burn-scarred arms ached from the reins, and he did not care. The walls were in sight. The walls were still standing. The gate was still—

A flash. A thunder. The golden dome that had shimmered over the capital for the past hour fractured into a thousand shards of dying light, and M'rak's heart stopped. He did not know what the dome was. He did not know how it had been raised or why it had fallen. But he knew, with the cold certainty of a soldier who had fought on the border for fifteen years, that it was bad.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<span class="speech">"RIDE FASTER!"</span> His voice was raw, hoarse, barely audible over the thunder of the Wohs' feet. <span class="speech">"THE WALLS ARE ABOUT TO BE BREACHED! RIDE FOR YOUR FAMILIES! RIDE FOR YOUR KING!"</span>
</div>

The Wohs surged beneath them, their great legs churning the sand, their AOE fields shimmering around their massive forms like heat haze. They were the desert variation—the Bright Paw garrison's most lethal weapon, bred to defend the eastern border against the Fire Beetles, their kinetic barriers capable of turning aside even the great insects' charges. They were exhausted. They were running on instinct and loyalty and the last reserves of their strength. They were almost there.

On the northern wall, the earth began to tremble. The Wengari defenders felt it through the stone—a low, rhythmic vibration that was not the pounding of the war drums, not the crash of Amuk fireballs, not the scrabbling of scorpion claws. Irek felt it through the broken scorpion claw he was still clutching in his bleeding hands. Sera felt it through the stone beneath her lacquered claws. The Pyrinae sorcerers felt it through their rune-glass staves.

They thought it was the walls collapsing. They thought the gate was finally giving way.

Then the war horn sounded. It was not a Humman horn. It was deep and resonant and ancient, the sound of a Bright Paw garrison horn that had been forged in the time of the Third Tyrant and had not been blown in battle for three hundred years. It echoed across the desert, across the wall, across the capital, and every Wengari who heard it knew what it meant.

In the war room, the elders looked up. Zara's golden eyes widened. Kareth's obsidian staff stopped its nervous tapping. Sylva, still seated at the edge of the granite table, her silver aura flickering, raised her ancient head. Vasha's humorless face did not change, but her cold eyes moved to the window. The horn sounded again, closer now, and the war room emptied as the elders rushed to the walls.

The Woh riders came over the eastern ridge like a golden tide. Twenty fully grown theropods, each one four meters tall at the shoulder and five meters from snout to tail, their natural plate armor gleaming in the morning sun, their AOE fields shimmering around them like heat haze. M'rak rode at their head, his golden armor dented and scorched, his burn-scarred arms raised, his voice carrying across the battlefield with the force of a war cry.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"DEPLOY THE FIRE FIELDS! BURN THEM OFF THE WALLS!"</p>
</div>

At his command, five Woh broke formation and charged toward the northern gate. Their AOE fields flared—shimmering domes of kinetic energy that expanded outward from their massive bodies, washing over the scorpions clinging to the wall. The Humman riders screamed as the fields struck them, their bodies thrown backward by the invisible force, their scorpions' claws losing their grip on the ancient stone.

They fell in waves—dozens of them, then scores, their iridescent shells crashing into the sand below. The Wengari on the wall cheered. It was not a controlled cheer. It was the raw, desperate roar of soldiers who had been fighting for three hours and had just watched salvation arrive.

The remaining fifteen Woh riders charged toward the Amuks. The great eight-legged war-beasts turned to face them, their tusks lowered, their artillery crews scrambling to reload. The Humman sorcerers on the Amuks' backs loosed volleys of elemental fire—ice and acid and flame—that struck the Wohs' plate armor and dissipated into nothing. The Woh did not slow. Their AOE fields flared, and the first rank of Amuks was simply swept aside, their legs buckling, their great bodies crashing into the sand.

The Humman artillery crews screamed as they fell. The second rank of Amuks tried to retreat, but the Woh were already among them—gravity-manipulating theropods from the west and AOE-field theropods from the east, their natural weapons carving through the Humman siege engines like claws through silk.

Only two Amuks managed to retreat. Their tusked forms stumbled back across the sand, their legs shaking, their crews clinging to the artillery platforms. The rest were dead or dying. The Humman sorcerers who survived pelted the Wohs with desperate volleys, but the great theropods simply shook off the attacks and pressed forward.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">M'rak raised his voice above the chaos, his golden armor blazing in the morning sun. "TO THE WALLS! DEFEND THE CITY! DEFEND OUR BROTHERS!"</p>
</div>

The Woh riders wheeled and charged toward the gate. The Wengari on the wall were still cheering, still fighting, still holding. And the great war, which had been moments from defeat, was suddenly, impossibly, not over.

The Humman command tent was no longer a place of strategy. It was a place of panic.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">Mekhmed the Third was screaming. His silk robes were drenched with sweat, his jeweled fingers trembling as they pointed at the battlefield beyond the tent flaps. "HOW CAN WE BE LOSING? WE HAD TEN AMUKS! TEN! YOU'RE ALL A BUNCH OF STUPIDS! WHAT DO WE HAVE LEFT? HOW DO WE KILL THOSE OVERGROWN CHICKENS?!"</p>
</div>

The generals stood in their loose semicircle, their faces no longer carefully neutral. The masks had slipped. What remained was exhaustion and disgust and the cold, bitter certainty of soldiers who had been ordered into a slaughter by a king who did not understand war.

<div class="dialogue-block">
Tamsin stepped forward. Her scarred face was set, her dark eyes burning with a fury she no longer bothered to hide. <span class="speech">"I told you this would happen. Those are the Wengari Wohs. I've fought them in the border. They're nigh immortal. Their plate armor turns aside scorpion stings and Amuk tusks alike. Their AOE fields can sweep entire formations off the field. Their gravity manipulation can pin a scorpion to the ground and crush it without a single claw being raised. I counted twenty Wohs, Your Wealthiness. Five of them are enough to level a city. They brought twenty."</span> She paused, letting the number settle. <span class="speech">"Do you understand that number? They have brought enough forces to level Verdantis five times over."</span>
</div>

The tent fell silent. The war drums still pounded outside, but they seemed distant now, irrelevant. Mekhmed stared at his general. His mouth opened and closed.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"Twenty," <span class="speech-attr">he repeated.</span></p>
</div>

<div class="dialogue-block">
<span class="speech">"Twenty. And those are only the Bright Paw garrison Wohs. The Stripe Paws have their own. The Shadow Paws have their own. The Motted Paws have their own. You marched nine hundred kilometers to conquer a city that has not fallen in three thousand years. You were told the White Dawn was dead. You were told the capital was defenseless. You were told a lie by a frightened scribe, and you believed it because you wanted to believe it. And now four hundred of our scorpion riders are dead, eight of our Amuks are dead, and the Wengari reinforcements are here."</span> She stepped closer, her scarred face inches from his. <span class="speech">"We should retreat. Now. While we still have an army to retreat with."</span>
</div>

The command tent was silent. The war drums still pounded outside, but inside, the only sound was the ragged breathing of a king who had run out of cleverness and was reaching for the only weapon he had left.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"Use the suicide scorpions."</p>
</div>

Mekhmed's voice was quiet now. The petulant fury of the earlier hours had burned away into something colder, something harder. His jeweled fingers were white-knuckled on the edge of the campaign table, and his dark eyes were fixed on the battlefield beyond the tent flaps, where the Woh riders were still sweeping through his army like a golden scythe.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">Tamsin stepped forward. Her scarred face was no longer set in the cold mask of a general who had been told to shut it. It was twisted with fury. "Haven't enough of our soldiers died already?! Those are our own riders! Our own men! You want to burn them alive for a tactical advantage that won't even—"</p>
</div>

<div class="dialogue-block">
<span class="speech">"Guards."</span> Mekhmed did not look at her. <span class="speech">"Remove her from her post. She is relieved of command. Deploy the suicide scorpions. Now."</span>
</div>

The guards hesitated. They were Humman, their dark eyes wide with the particular terror of soldiers who had just been ordered to arrest their own general. Tamsin stared at Mekhmed for a long, cold moment. Then she turned and walked out of the tent without waiting for the guards to touch her. Her back was straight. Her scarred face was set. She did not speak. She did not need to. The other generals watched her go, and their silence was a kind of oath.

Outside, the Woh riders were beginning to relax. M'rak had dismounted—or was about to dismount—his burn-scarred arms aching, his golden armor dented and scorched, his heart still pounding with the exhilaration of the charge. The walls were clear. The gate was holding. The Amuks were routed. He had done what he came to do. He had saved the city. He was thinking, in the quiet space behind his thoughts, that he might actually live to see the sun set.

Then fifty scorpion riders broke from the Humman lines and charged.

It was not a coordinated assault. The riders were scattered, their formations loose, their mounts moving at a lumbering pace that was almost leisurely. The Wohs watched them come, and the Wengari on their backs laughed—a rough, disbelieving laugh, the laughter of warriors who had just swept aside ten Amuks and were now being asked to swat away a handful of stragglers.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<span class="speech">"Last ditch,"</span> someone shouted. <span class="speech">"They're sending their last riders to die!"</span>
</div>

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">M'rak did not laugh. Something was wrong. The riders were moving too slowly. Their formations were too loose. They were not trying to engage. They were trying to reach a position. "Get back! Get the Wohs back!"</p>
</div>

It was too late.

The scorpions reached the cluster of Woh riders standing at the farthest edge of the battlefield, where the Wohs had pursued the retreating Amuks and were now isolated from the wall. The riders closed their eyes. And pillars of fire erupted from the sand.

They were not fireballs. They were not the controlled, sorcery-enhanced volleys that the Amuk artillery crews had been firing at the wall for hours. They were something else entirely—something ancient, something desperate, something that consumed the scorpion and the rider and the air itself in a column of flame ten meters tall and three meters wide. The sand beneath them turned to glass. The Wohs caught in the blast died instantly—their natural plate armor no defense against the heat, their AOE fields flickering out like guttering candles.

The Wengari on their backs died with them. The survivors—the Wohs at the edge of the blast radius, the riders who had been close enough to feel the heat but not close enough to be consumed—reeled backward, their mounts screaming, their golden manes singed and smoking.

M'rak stared at the pillars of fire. His burn-scarred arms, already marked by fifteen years of Fire Beetle engagements, hung limp at his sides. He had seen Wengari die before. He had seen Wohs die before. He had never seen his own soldiers consumed by a weapon that killed the enemy alongside them. He had never seen anything like this.

On the wall, the cheering had stopped.

Outside the war room, Vasha had Seris by the throat. The Humman ambassador's back was pressed against the stone wall, her diplomatic composure shattered, her dark eyes wide with terror. Vasha's humorless face was inches from hers, her cold eyes burning with a fury that was not cold at all.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"WHAT WAS THAT? There isn't an explosive that can do that. I would know. I have studied every weapon, every device, every scrap of sorcery that the Hummans possess. There is no explosive that produces a ten-meter pillar of fire and leaves the sand turned to glass. WHAT WAS THAT?"</p>
</div>

Seris could not speak. Vasha's grip was too tight. Her dark eyes were rolling, her hands scrabbling uselessly at the Stripe Paw's claws.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">Sylva stepped forward. Her silver aura was still flickering, still guttering, but her ancient voice was steady. "Release her, Vasha. She cannot answer if you crush her throat."</p>
</div>

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">Vasha held Seris for a moment longer. Then she released her, and the Humman ambassador crumpled to the stone floor, gasping for breath. Vasha looked down at her. "Tell us. Now. Or I will find someone else who can, and I will not be gentle."</p>
</div>

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">Seris raised her head. Her voice was hoarse, barely a whisper. "I don't know. I don't know what that was. I have never seen it. I did not know we had such a weapon. I did not know any of this. My daughter—my daughter wrote the letters. I did not know."</p>
</div>

The war room was silent. The pillars of fire were still burning in the distance, reflected in the windows, casting long, dancing shadows across the granite table. The Woh riders had been reduced. The Humman army was still fighting. And the great war was not yet over.

On the northern wall, M'rak stood amid the aftermath of the fire pillars and tried to breathe. Five Wohs remained healthy. Five. Out of twenty. The others were dead—consumed by the columns of flame that had erupted from the sand, their natural plate armor no defense against whatever sorcery the Hummans had unleashed. The Wengari riders who had been caught in the blasts were dead alongside them. The sand where the pillars had risen was still smoking, still glowing faintly with the residual heat, patches of it fused into rough, blackened glass.

M'rak stared at those patches, and something stirred in the back of his mind. A memory. A story. Something about the Fifth Tyrant. Something about Velarius Vane. But the thought would not surface. It hovered at the edge of his consciousness, familiar and terrible and just out of reach.

<div class="dialogue-block">
In the Humman command tent, Mekhmed was smiling. It was not a pleasant smile. <span class="speech">"Serves them right. Stupid brutes. Did you see the way they just stood there? They thought they had won. They thought we were finished."</span> He shook his head slowly, his jeweled fingers tapping against the campaign table. <span class="speech">"They don't know what we have. They don't know what we've been keeping."</span>
</div>

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">The generals stood in their loose semicircle, their faces pale. Tamsin was gone—relieved of command, escorted from the tent by guards who had looked at the floor rather than meet her eyes. The remaining generals were younger, less experienced, more afraid of their king than they were of the enemy. One of them—a nervous man with a scar across his cheek—spoke up. "Your Wealthiness... what was that? We were not briefed on any weapon of that magnitude. The scorpions were rigged with—"</p>
</div>

<div class="dialogue-block">
<span class="speech">"Something Velarius left for a time like this."</span> Mekhmed's smile widened. <span class="speech">"The Fifth Tyrant was a monster, but he was our monster. And he left us many things. Some of them are still useful."</span>
</div>

The generals stared at him. The scarred man opened his mouth, then closed it. No one spoke. The war drums pounded on outside, low and patient, but the tent was very still. Velarius Vane had been dead for five hundred years. His Plague had lasted sixty-five. The Scar still pulsed with light on certain nights. And his king was smiling about it.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">On the wall, a sentry shouted. "Rider! Rider approaching from the Humman lines! Single rider, Tide Wolf mount, full gallop!"</p>
</div>

M'rak turned, his hand going to the broken scorpion claw at his belt—the only weapon he had left. The other defenders on the wall braced for another attack. But the rider was alone. No scorpion vanguard. No Amuk artillery. No suicide charges. Just a single Humman on a Tide Wolf, riding hard toward the gate with something clutched in one upraised hand.

As the rider drew closer, the defenders could make out more details. The rider was a woman. Her dark skin was cross-hatched with scars. Her uniform was that of a Humman general, but she wore no helmet, and her hands were empty of weapons. She was shouting something, her voice lost to the wind and the distance, and the Tide Wolf beneath her was lathered with sweat, its great sides heaving. She was not attacking. She was fleeing.

Tamsin stumbled from the saddle of the Tide Wolf, her scarred hands raised high above her head, her dark eyes wild with desperation. She had ridden through the Humman lines, past the remnants of the scorpion vanguard, past the shattered Amuks, past the still-smoking craters where the suicide scorpions had detonated. No one had tried to stop her. Perhaps they had not recognized her. Perhaps they had recognized her and let her go.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"SOMEONE IN COMMAND! SOMEONE IN COMMAND! I'M TAMSIN, GENERAL OF THE HUMMANS! PLEASE, YOU MUST SAVE US! HE HAS—HE HAS VELARIUS MADNESS!"</p>
</div>

M'rak's face lost its color. The memory that had been stirring at the edge of his thoughts since the fire pillars erupted snapped into place with terrible clarity. The glassed sand. The ten-meter columns of flame. The way the Wohs' plate armor had melted like wax. He had heard the old veterans describe it. He had seen the illustrations in the border garrison's archives. Plague weapons. The Fifth Tyrant's arsenal.

The things Velarius Vane had built during the sixty-five years of his reign, when he poisoned the world and burned the sky. The things that had been buried with him. The things that were supposed to have been destroyed.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"EVERYONE STAY CLEAR FROM THE DEAD! THEY CARRY VELARIUS PLAGUE!"</p>
</div>

The words ripped through the defenders on the wall like a windstorm through dry grass. The young Bright Paw guard beside him—Irek, whose shoulder was still bleeding from the scorpion claw he had swung—stared at the smoking craters with new horror. The Pyrinae sorcerers, still chanting their ice spells, faltered. The elite unit, still forming up behind Sera, froze in their tracks. The Plague. The word carried more weight than any scorpion charge, any Amuk artillery, any suicide rider.

The Plague had lasted sixty-five years. The Plague had killed eight percent of Ethra's population. The Plague had been born from Velarius Vane, and it had taken a massive sacrifice of Styx and Rune Wolves to end it. If the Hummans had somehow preserved a fragment of that ancient horror, if they had weaponized it, then the battle was no longer about the gate or the wall or the city. It was about survival.

On the sand before the wall, a second contingent of suicide scorpions was already marching. Fifty more riders, their mounts moving at that same slow, deliberate pace, their dark eyes closed, their hands gripping the reins with the resignation of men who had already accepted their deaths. They were heading for the wall this time—not for the Wohs, not for the defenders, but for the gate itself. And if the pillars of fire erupted against the ancient stone, if the Plague seeped into the city, if the same sorcery that had turned the Wohs to ash reached the temple where the common Wengari were sheltering, reached the royal chamber where Kira was curled in a corner with her scorpions, reached the bed where Ajani lay unconscious in his golden armor, then the battle would not be lost.

It would be the end of everything.
