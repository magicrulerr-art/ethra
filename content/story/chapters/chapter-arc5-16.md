## Chapter 16: 11:50 — Vows Are Absolved

At 11:50, the temple summoned Solen. On the wall, M'rak was losing minutes he did not have.

The temple had become a war council. Solen stood at the center of the great hall, his golden mane dulled by exhaustion, his ancient eyes hollow from the long morning of death and desperation. Around him, the wounded lay on their pallets, the children huddled in clusters, the elders who were too old to fight sat in silence. And before him stood a broad-shouldered Bright Paw warrior whose golden mane was streaked with grey and whose ceremonial armor was still stained with the blood of the second vanguard.

He had fought on the wall since dawn. He had watched his comrades die. He had carried their bodies to the temple and returned to the wall and fought again. Now he was here, and his voice was steady.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"Solen. Absolve us from our vows. It's time."</p>
</div>

Solen looked at him. The vows of a Bright Paw warrior were sworn to the Lightbringer, to the pact, to the Golden Sun. They were not merely words; they were the foundation of the royal family's identity. To absolve a warrior from his vows was to release him from his duty to survive, to return home, to protect his kin in the ordered way of the temple and the throne. It was a declaration that there was no more home to return to. It was a permission to die.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">Solen stood. His ancient joints ached, his golden mane hung limp, his voice was hoarse from hours of prayer. But he stood. "Then I go with you."</p>
</div>

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">The broad-shouldered warrior stared at him. "Solen—you are the High Priest. If you fall—"</p>
</div>

<div class="dialogue-block">
<span class="speech">"If the city falls, there will be no High Priest."</span> Solen's voice was quiet but absolute. <span class="speech">"I am old. I have served the Lightbringer for longer than most of you have been alive. I have kept the old prayers and the old rituals, and I have watched the Bright Paws grow weak while the other families grew strong. I will not die in this temple while our warriors die on the wall."</span> He raised his head, and something flickered in his ancient eyes—not hope, not quite, but something close to resolve. <span class="speech">"Absolve me from my vows. I absolve you from yours. Now let us go."</span>
</div>

On the wall, M'rak was watching the black thing approach. It was barely two leagues away now, its eight legs moving with an increasing, terrible steadiness. The purple orbs where its eyes should have been were fixed on the gate, and the green barbs had stopped firing—the creature was conserving its ammunition, or it had exhausted it, or it simply wanted to tear the wall apart with its claws. M'rak did not know which, and he did not have time to care.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<span class="speech">"How long for the ray?!"</span> The Hydromancer at the mirror array controls, her copper skin slick with sweat, her rune-glass staff trembling in her hands, answered without looking up. <span class="speech">"Three minutes! The crystals are nearly spent! Three minutes, and then—"</span>
</div>

<div class="dialogue-block">
<span class="speech">"We don't have three minutes."</span> M'rak turned to Yvaria, who was still aloft on her ghost, her drums still strapped to her chest. <span class="speech">"Pelt it with arrows! Slow it down! Anything!"</span>
</div>

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">Zephyr was already moving. He raised his lacquered claws and shouted to his riders. "CHARGE! THE WOLVES! THE FIRE! SLOW IT DOWN!"</p>
</div>

The black Tide Wolves surged forward, their riders crouched low on their backs, their shadow flares igniting in sheets of dark flame. They hit the creature like a wave of fire, their wolves biting and tearing at its legs, their riders slashing at its segmented body with their lacquered claws. The shadow flare burned through the creature's chitin, and for a moment—a brief, desperate moment—the creature seemed to stagger.

But every wound healed within seconds. The chitin knit itself together as fast as the wolves could tear it. The shadow fire guttered and died against the creature's flesh, and the wolves were forced back, their jaws bloody, their riders exhausted.

The ghosts loosed their arrows. Yvaria's drummers beat out a frantic rhythm, and the remaining ghosts fired volley after volley into the creature's eyes, its mouth, its joints. The arrows struck true, but they did nothing. The creature did not slow. It did not pause. It simply kept walking, its eight legs churning the sand, its purple eyes fixed on the gate.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">Zephyr pulled his wolves back, his scarred face twisted with frustration. "Nothing! The wounds don't stay! It's not even slowing!"</p>
</div>

M'rak watched the creature take another step. And another. It was barely a league and a half away now, and it was still coming, and there was nothing left to throw at it. The rune cannons were still recharging. The mirror array was still counting down. The defenders on the wall were still standing, but they were standing with broken claws and empty hands and the hollow, distant stares of people who had been fighting for almost seven hours and knew they were about to die.

Then, from behind him, a new sound rose above the chaos. It was not the roar of cannons or the scream of ghosts or the thunder of charging wolves. It was the slow, steady march of paws on stone. M'rak turned. Solen was walking toward the gate, his golden mane still dulled by exhaustion, his ceremonial robes still stained with the dust of the temple. And behind him, in a long, silent column, walked the Bright Paw priests.

They wore no armor. They carried no weapons. Their golden manes were unbraided, their ceremonial robes were torn, and their ancient eyes were fixed on the black thing approaching the wall.

The broad-shouldered warrior who had asked for absolution walked at Solen's side. He had found a spear somewhere—an old, ceremonial weapon that had been hanging on the temple wall—and he was holding it with the steady, practiced grip of someone who had been a soldier before he was a priest.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<span class="speech">"The ray needs three minutes,"</span> Solen said, his voice carrying across the shattered rampart. <span class="speech">"We will give you three minutes. We will give you everything we have left."</span> He raised his head, and his ancient voice rang out across the wall. <span class="speech">"FOR THE LIGHTBRINGER! FOR THE GOLDEN SUN! FOR THE WHITE DAWN!"</span>
</div>

The Bright Paw priests raised their ceremonial spears and their broken claws and their ancient, weary voices, and they marched toward the gate. They were not warriors. They were old men and old women, scholars and healers and keepers of ritual. But they were Wengari. And the Wengari did not run.

It was 11:50 in the morning, the seventh day of the Month of Storms, in the first year of the reign of Ajani Brightmane. The wall was broken. The defenders were spent. The black thing was almost at the gate. And the priests of the Bright Paws were walking out to meet it.
