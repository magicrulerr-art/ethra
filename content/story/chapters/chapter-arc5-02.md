## Chapter 2: 06:25 — Sera Holds The Gate

At 6:25, Sera held the gate.

Sera stood at the gate, her ruined face impassive, her lacquered claws dripping with the blood of the second vanguard. Around her, the elite unit was still breathing hard, their dark pelts matted with sweat and sand and the ichor of scorpions. Four of them were wounded—one grievously, her arm hanging limp—but all twenty still stood. They had held. They had won.

And then she saw the third wave.

Four hundred scorpion riders. The entire remaining vanguard of the Humman army, stretching across the horizon in a sea of iridescent shells and gleaming claws. Behind them, the Amuks were forming a line, their great tusked forms swaying in unison, their eight-legged strides eating up the distance between the desert and the gate. The Tide Wolf cavalry was circling to the east, fifty massive wolves with riders in striped armor, waiting for the moment when the gate would fall and the slaughter would begin.

*We can't win against four hundred. We barely survived fifty.*

The thought was cold and clear and utterly without panic. Sera had been an assassin for decades. She had learned to count enemies before she learned to read. She knew when a battle was winnable and when it was not. This battle was not winnable. Not with twenty warriors. Not with the cannons still recharging. Not with the hawks and the killer claws already spent.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"FALL BACK! FALL BACK TO THE GATES!"</p>
</div>

Her voice carried across the courtyard, sharp and commanding. The elite unit obeyed without hesitation—they had trained for months under her, had learned to trust her instincts as their own. They retreated through the great bronze doors, their claws scraping against the stone, their wounded carried between them. The Bright Paw guards on the wall loosed a volley of arrows down into the advancing scorpions, but the shafts bounced harmlessly off their iridescent shells.

The scorpions were climbing now, their great claws finding purchase in the ancient stone, their segmented legs scrabbling against the walls.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"FIRE THE CANNONS!" <span class="speech-attr">Sera shouted.</span></p>
</div>

But the cannons were not reloaded. The Pyrinae sorcerers were still chanting, their rune-glass staves humming with stored energy, but the runic sequences took time—twenty minutes between shots, if the wind was favorable, if the sorcerers were skilled. Only two cannons fired. One missed entirely, its white-hot blast carving a useless furrow in the empty sand. The other struck the flank of the third wave, vaporizing fifty riders in a cone of light. Fifty gone. Three hundred and fifty remained.

The scorpions reached the wall. They began to climb, their claws punching through the ancient stone, their riders clinging to their backs. The Bright Paw guards on the parapets fell back, their spears useless against the armored carapaces. Irek was among them—the young Brightmane soldier who had watched the first wave die with hope in his heart was now watching the third wave crest the wall, and his spear was shaking in his hands.

Then the Pyrinae sorcerers began to chant. A different chant this time—not the deep, resonant hum of the rune cannons, but something older, something colder. The rune-glass staves along the wall began to glow with a pale blue light, and a sheet of ice spread across the ancient stone. It was not natural ice. It was sorcery—water pulled from the desert air, flash-frozen by runic sequences that the Pyrinae had perfected over three thousand years of serving the Wengari.

The scorpions' claws slipped. Their segmented legs scrabbled for purchase and found none. One by one, they fell, crashing back into the sand below, their riders screaming.

For a moment, the wall was clear. For a moment, Sera allowed herself to breathe.

Then the Amuks formed a line. Ten of them, five meters tall, eight-legged, their tusks four meters wide and gleaming like polished ivory. They lowered their heads as one, and a voice—a Humman voice, amplified by sorcery—echoed across the battlefield.

The Amuks' backs were rigged with platforms, and on those platforms sat Humman artillery crews. They carried long, cylindrical tubes of polished metal, their surfaces etched with runic sequences that glowed a deep, angry red. The tubes were aimed at the wall, at the gate, at the Wengari defenders who had just survived the scorpion assault and were now staring at something they had never seen before.

Volleys of fireballs drew parabolas over the air. They were not cannons—not in the way the Pyrinae understood cannons. They were smaller, more precise, more numerous. Each Amuk carried four tubes, and each tube fired a sphere of compressed, sorcery-enhanced flame that arced over the desert and crashed into the wall with a roar of fire and shattered stone.

The first volley struck the parapets. Young Wengari—Bright Paws, mostly, the guards who had been stationed on the wall since dawn—were engulfed in flame. Irek saw a soldier he had trained with, a young female with a golden mane and a quick laugh, disappear in a burst of fire. He saw another fall from the wall, her body trailing smoke as she tumbled into the sand below. He heard screaming—Wengari screaming, Pyrinae screaming—and he realized, with a cold, distant clarity, that one of the screams was his own.

The second volley struck the Pyrinae sorcerers. Nefere's chanters had been maintaining the ice on the walls, their staves glowing with pale blue light, their voices steady and calm. The fireballs crashed into their ranks, and the ice shattered. The sorcerers fell—some dead, some burning, some simply gone. The ice on the walls cracked and melted, and the scorpions began to climb again.

Sera stood at the gate, her lacquered claws still dripping, her ruined face lit by the glow of the burning wall. The third wave was three hundred and fifty strong. The Amuks were reloading. The Tide Wolves were circling. The cannons were silent. The hawks were spent. The killer claws were dead.

'We cannot hold.'

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">But she was a Shadow Paw. She had been trained to hold impossible positions. She raised her claws and shouted, her voice carrying across the chaos, "ELITE UNIT! TO THE BREACH! WE HOLD THE GATE OR WE DIE AT THE GATE! THERE IS NO RETREAT!"</p>
</div>

The elite unit formed up behind her. Twenty warriors against three hundred and fifty. The wall was burning. The gate was cracking. And it was 6:25 in the morning, the seventh day of the Month of Storms, in the first year of the reign of Ajani Brightmane, first of his name. The White Dawn lay unconscious in his chambers, the golden armor still pulsing faintly over his chest. And the great war was about to be lost.

The eastern garrison was three hundred leagues from the capital, a sprawling fortress of sandstone and rune-glass that guarded the border against the Fire Beetles. It was manned by Bright Paws—the royal family's soldiers, stationed far from the comforts of the capital, their golden manes bleached pale by the desert sun. They had not seen a war in decades. They had not seen their king since his coronation.

But they had received a message from Toren, the old scribe on the Council of the Untrustworthy, and they had mobilized before the elders even knew the Humman army existed.

M'rak led them. He was thirty years old, young for a general, his golden mane cropped short, his arms covered in the burn scars of a hundred Fire Beetle engagements. He wore full golden armor—not ceremonial, but battle-worn, dented and scratched and polished to a high sheen. He was smiling. The Woh beneath him was a desert variation, its natural plate armor gleaming in the pre-dawn darkness, its AOE field shimmering faintly around its massive form like a heat haze.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">A younger Wengari rode beside him, his golden mane still soft with youth, his eyes wide with the particular terror of someone who had never ridden to war. "General, this wasn't approved! Are you sure there will be an invasion? The elders didn't authorize—"</p>
</div>

<div class="dialogue-block">
<span class="speech">"Toren sent word."</span> M'rak's voice was calm, almost cheerful. <span class="speech">"He's a bastard, but you can trust he'll want to live. If he says the capital needs us, then we march. Our king lies ill. You did not see him at the ceremony, or the tournament. He's a force of nature. All we need to do is endure until he wakes."</span>
</div>

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">The younger Wengari hesitated. The Woh thundered beneath them, its great strides eating up the desert. "M'rak... how do you know he will wake?"</p>
</div>

<div class="dialogue-block">
M'rak turned in his saddle. The smile did not leave his face, but his eyes were very serious. <span class="speech">"Because before he's a king, he's a Wengari. Wengari don't run from fights."</span> He raised his voice, a roar that carried across the line of riders. <span class="speech">"NOW RIDE FASTER!"</span>
</div>

The western garrison was a damp river basin where the Rune-Belt frayed into the Flickermarch, a place of mist and ancient trees and the distant howling of Tide Wolves. The Stripe Paws had guarded this border for thousands of years, their Woh riders patrolling the edges of the March, their caravans carrying trade goods through the one safe pass. General Reva commanded them. She was a veteran of forty years, her striped mane streaked with grey, her golden eyes sharp and calculating.

She had received Vasha's message three weeks ago, and she had been marching ever since.

Her Woh was drinking from the river, its massive head lowered, its natural plate armor glistening with moisture. Reva stood beside it, studying a map by the light of a small sorcery-enhanced lantern. The map showed the northern approach to the capital, the desert roads, the estimated position of the Humman army. The numbers were not good.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">A young Stripe Paw officer approached, his expression uncertain. "General, can you trust Vasha? I heard she once sold a bag full of rocks for three months' allowance to a merchant."</p>
</div>

<div class="dialogue-block">
Reva laughed—a genuine, booming sound that echoed across the river basin. <span class="speech">"Of course you can't trust her. That's why we must go. Vasha said the Hummans think like her, and if that's true, you can count on those weak pests to try something. We need to move at full pace. We must make it before dawn."</span> She rolled up the map and mounted her Woh in a single fluid motion. <span class="speech">"Relay the orders. We leave at once."</span>
</div>

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">She spurred her mount forward, the great theropod rising to its full height and then charging into the darkness. Behind her, the officer shouted, "General Reva has spoken! Move your fat behinds and ride!"</p>
</div>

Twenty-five Woh riders thundered out of the river basin and into the desert, their gravity-manipulating auras already beginning to pulse.

The ghosts flew in silence. A hundred of them, their five-meter wingspans blotting out the stars, their bodies flickering in and out of visibility as their optical camouflage pulsed with the rhythm of the drums. The drums were constant—a deep, resonant beat that traveled through the bone rather than the air, the language of the Motted Paws and their ancient allies. The ghosts responded to the drums, their formations shifting with each new rhythm, their silent wings carrying them toward the capital.

General Yvaria led them. She was young, barely twenty-five, her silver-furred face alight with fierce determination. She rode at the head of the formation on the largest ghost, her drum harness strapped across her chest, her paws beating out a constant, urgent rhythm. The ghosts were tired. They had been flying for hours, their wing muscles aching, their optical camouflage flickering with exhaustion.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">A Stripe Paw drummer rode beside her, his own drum strapped to his saddle, his face pale with fatigue. "The ghosts say they are tired, General. We should rest."</p>
</div>

<div class="dialogue-block">
<span class="speech">"Absolutely not!"</span> Yvaria's voice was bright, almost giddy. <span class="speech">"Vasha sent word to the Stripe Paws, but I sent word to the Motted Paws too. We won't be outshone!"</span>
</div>

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">The drummer shook his head. "But General, the elders didn't approve! We didn't even tell them!"</p>
</div>

<div class="dialogue-block">
<span class="speech">"The elders love to sit on their high chairs and tell us to listen. Well, I'm fed up of listening. You didn't see the king in the arena—he was glorious! And I'm going to be his bride!"</span> She laughed, wild and bright, and spurred her ghost faster. <span class="speech">"Now, faster! We need to be there before dawn! Faster!"</span>
</div>

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">The drummer sighed. "Yvaria, you say the same of every male that suits your fancy. Last month you were going to marry Virya from the River Clan!"</p>
</div>

Yvaria just laughed and rode ahead, her drums beating faster, her ghost's wings cutting through the night.

The shadow riders moved like a storm. A hundred black Tide Wolves, their dense fur absorbing the moonlight, their bodies engulfed in the faint, flickering remnants of the shadow flare—the parasitic fire that the First Tyrant had unleashed on the southern belt five thousand years ago. The wolves did not howl. They ran in silence, their massive paws churning the sand into a cloud that rose behind them like a sandstorm.

The Wengari on their backs were Shadow Paws, their dark pelts blending with the wolves' fur, their lacquered claws gleaming in the darkness.

Zephyr led them. He was young—perhaps twenty-two—his scarred face still bearing the marks of the tournament where he had fought and lost in the first phase. He had not forgotten. He had not forgiven himself. And when Maren's message reached Xhilva, he had been the first to volunteer.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<span class="speech">"OUR KING NEEDS US!"</span> His voice was raw, hoarse from shouting over the thunder of the wolves' paws. <span class="speech">"MAREN SAID SO! WE WILL NOT FAIL THE WHITE DAWN! WE WILL NOT FAIL THE ONLY KING WHO'S SHOWN US RESPECT SINCE THE BLACK FIRE! HE EVEN ADOPTED A CUB OF US AND MADE HER HIS SISTER!"</span>
</div>

The wolves surged forward, their shadow flares intensifying, the fire licking at their dark fur without consuming them. The Shadow Paws on their backs raised their lacquered claws and roared.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"RIDE! RIDE OR I WILL PERSONALLY SLAUGHTER YOU ALL! RIDE!"</p>
</div>

They rode. East, west, south—they rode toward the capital, toward the gate, toward the battle that was already raging. The Woh riders thundered across the desert. The ghosts flew through the darkness. The shadow riders surged like a black tide. And the first light of dawn was just beginning to touch the horizon.
