## Chapter 22: 12:06 — The White Dawn Wakes

Ajani reached for the fifth page. His left arm was still coiled by the fire serpent, his neck still ringed in ice, his left leg still swirling with tiny white stars, his right shoulder still pierced by the golden Styx's talons. Blood ran from his nose and dripped onto the breastplate of the Lightbringer's armor. His voice, when he began to chant, was strained and hoarse, each word an effort dragged from a body that was running out of time.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"Golden Spirit from the deep, the one who guards the gate, keeper of riches untold, this humble sorcerer beseeches you—take from me and answer my plea. Be made manifest and answer my plea. I call to thee—AURUM!"</p>
</div>

His right leg was engulfed in a golden, gooey substance that pulsed and shifted, changing form and density with each passing second. The earth spear—the last of the five—dissolved into golden powder that floated upward, drifting on a wind that did not exist, and reformed above the elemental avatars. A halo of pure gold materialized above each of them, spinning slowly, casting long, dancing shadows across the sand.

Three pairs of golden wings erupted from their backs—wings of light and metal and earth, each feather a blade, each joint a perfect articulation of the ancient sorcery that bound them. The black armor that the dark Ajani had gifted them began to shimmer with golden motifs, intricate patterns that depicted Wengari battles: the War of 3400, the border skirmishes of 3600, the great beetle invasion of the 4500th Tide.

The history of the Wengari, etched in gold onto the armor of the spirits who were fighting for their survival.

The elemental avatars arranged themselves at the cardinal points—fire at the east, ice at the south, light at the west, darkness at the north, and the golden spirit above them all, its halo spinning. They drove their weapons into the sand: the twin sabers, the scepter, the two-handed sword, the dark spear, and a new weapon—a golden staff that the Aurum spirit had brought with it from the deep. A wall of light erupted from the weapons, a pentagon of elemental fury that trapped the creature within.

The creature screamed and hurled itself against the barrier. Cracks formed instantly, spider-webbing across the golden surface. But the golden spirit's halo spun faster, and the cracks sealed themselves. The creature struck again. The cracks formed and sealed. Again. Again.

Everyone who had been chanting felt the drain. The ghosts, their drums silent, began to land on the shattered rampart, their riders slumping in their saddles. The people in the temple, who had been repeating the chants with the Veylar Shell-Singer, began to faint—old Wengari, young Humman, Bright Paw and Stripe Paw and Shadow Paw alike, their bodies no longer able to sustain the energy the chants demanded.

On the wall, soldiers fell to their knees. Zephyr's Tide Wolves collapsed onto their bellies, their shadow fires guttering. Yvaria's ghost settled onto the stone, its great wings folding, its rider's head bowed. Cefiro watched it all with an expression of awe and confusion, his pale blue eyes reflecting the golden light of the barrier, his notebook still forgotten in his paw.

Kira clutched Pearl tightly against her chest. The white scorpion's antennae were waving frantically, and Black Fire and Red Fire pressed themselves against Kira's legs, their small bodies vibrating with the same exhaustion that was sweeping through the city.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">Ajani reached for the sixth page. Cefiro turned to Nefere, his voice barely a whisper. "There are no more spears."</p>
</div>

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">Nefere's clouded eyes were fixed on the sky. "I know."</p>
</div>

The creature screamed one last time and lost its form. The massive, eight-legged body of chitinous black flesh collapsed inward, folding and crumpling and dissolving. But it did not die. A pillar of pure blackness rose from its remains—a column of darkness so absolute that it seemed to punch a hole in the noon sky. The ground trembled. Fractures raced across the sand, spreading outward from the pillar's base.

And in the sky, black circles began to form—perfect, geometric voids that absorbed the light of the Steadfast and gave nothing back.

It was 12:06 in the afternoon. The battle had entered its final, terrible phase.

Ajani stared at the sixth page. The runes were already forming—golden, green, blue, red, white, and black—spiraling across the paper in concentric circles that seemed to move of their own accord. His body was failing. The fire serpent still constricted his right arm. The ice ring still tightened around his neck. The dark spirit's stars still swirled in his left leg. The golden Aurum substance still pulsed around his right.

The Styx talons still buried in his shoulder. Blood dripped from his nose and now from his mouth as well, a thin trickle of red that splashed against the breastplate.

'This is it. I barely have ten seconds, I think. I hope it's enough.'

He began to write. The grimoire's pages had never resisted him before, but this one seemed to push back, as if the words he was inscribing were too large for the paper to contain. The runes spiraled faster. He coughed blood onto the page, and the blood sizzled and burned with golden light.

He began to chant. His voice was barely a whisper now, each word dragged from a body that was almost spent.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"Guardians of the world who watch upon us, seated at the gates of knowledge, wisdom, virtue, power and will. Guardians of the world who judge upon us all—earth, fire, light, darkness, water..."</p>
</div>

The elemental avatars looked up. The fire Ajani, still gripping its twin sabers. The ice Ajani, still holding its scepter. The light Ajani, still carrying its two-handed sword. The dark Ajani, still wielding its spear. The golden Aurum spirit, still spinning its halo above them all. They raised their heads toward the sky, toward the king who had summoned them, toward the chant that was binding them together.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"Guardians of the world who care for us all, lend me your power and vanquish my foe. I call upon the primal guardians of the world! I call upon the unchanging masters of the arts!"</p>
</div>

The avatars raised one hand each toward the sky. The runes that had been spiraling on the page broke free, shooting upward in a column of multicolored light that pierced the black circles in the sky. The black pillar that had risen from the creature's remains sizzled and bubbled. The black mass hurled itself against the light cage, screaming, desperate, trying to escape what it knew was coming.

Ajani continued chanting. His voice was failing, but the chant carried on, carried by the avatars, carried by the city, carried by something older than all of them.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"Lend me your power to guard my home! I call upon the destroyer, the lightning that burns with inextinguishable glow! I call upon the storm that wreathes the world! Hear my chant and answer my plea! I call to thee—RAITEN!"</p>
</div>

The world went white. Every spirit vanished—fire, ice, light, darkness, gold—consumed in the same instant. The golden wall that had trapped the creature dissolved. The black mass, freed from its cage, lunged toward the wall with a scream of triumph. It did not reach the gate. From the sky, a dark cloud formed impossibly fast, blotting out the noon sun. A single thunderbolt struck the black mass in midair, stopping it cold.

Then another. Then another. Then a thousand bolts in the span of a few seconds, each one a pillar of pure, white-hot lightning that burned with inextinguishable glow. The black mass vaporized. There was nothing left—no chitin, no ichor, no purple Plague light. Only a dark scar on the sand where the lightning had struck.

Ajani fell. The golden wings on his back flickered and died. The fire serpent uncoiled from his arm and dissolved into embers. The ice ring shattered and melted away. The dark stars in his leg winked out. The golden Aurum substance evaporated. The Styx talons withdrew, and the spirit was gone. He fell, his body limp, his armor still glowing faintly, his eyes closed.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">Yvaria was already moving. Her ghost dove through the chaos, its great wings folded tight against its body, and she caught Ajani in midair. Her arms wrapped around the White Dawn, cradling him against the ghost's chest. The drums on her harness were silent. Her rune-glass communication shell was dark. But her voice, when she spoke, was steady. "I have him. He's alive. The king is alive."</p>
</div>

On the shattered wall, M'rak lowered his claws. Zephyr's Tide Wolves collapsed onto their bellies. Solen and the Golden Cloaks, their light pillars spent, sank to their knees. Nefere leaned on her cracked staff and closed her eyes. Cefiro stood motionless, his notebook still forgotten in his paw, his pale blue eyes reflecting the fading light of the vanished storm. Kira clutched Pearl against her chest and ran toward the gate, Black Fire and Red Fire scrambling at her heels.

On the seventh day of the Month of Storms, in the first year of the reign of King Ajani Brightmane, the battle that would be known as the Plague War was finally won. The White Dawn had woken. The creature was destroyed. And the sun of the Steadfast, warm and unforgiving and unflinching, shone down upon the shattered capital and the blackened sand and the bodies of the fallen and the living alike. The city had survived. The king had returned. And the great war was over.
