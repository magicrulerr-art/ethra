## Chapter 12: 10:35 — The Wall Blanketed

It was 10:35 in the morning, the seventh day of the Month of Storms, in the first year of the reign of Ajani Brightmane. The wall was blanketed in Plague-light. The Humman army was shattered, its king dead, its soldiers caught in the same net they had unleashed. The Wengari defenders were trapped inside their own city. And the true enemy—the one who had given Mekhmed the weapons, the one who had been waiting for this moment since the First Tyrant fell—was finally showing its face.

The shadow riders came over the northern ridge like a black tide. A hundred Tide Wolves, their dense fur absorbing the morning light, their bodies engulfed in the faint, flickering remnants of the shadow flare—the parasitic fire that the First Tyrant had unleashed on the southern belt five thousand years ago. The wolves did not howl. They ran in silence, their massive paws churning the sand into a cloud that rose behind them like a storm.

The Shadow Paws on their backs were dark shapes against the dark sky, their lacquered claws gleaming with the same purple and green poisons that Vex and Lira had carried.

Zephyr rode at their head. He was young, his scarred face still bearing the marks of the tournament where he had fought and lost in the first phase. He had not forgotten. He had not forgiven himself. And when Maren's message reached Xhilva, he had been the first to volunteer. Now he crested the ridge and saw the purple net.

It stretched across the northern approach to the capital like a wound in the sky. Four orbs, each one pulsing with sickly violet light, their tendrils woven together into a curtain of horror that blanketed the entire area before the wall. The screams were what hit him first—not the sight, not the scale, but the sound. Humman voices, Wengari voices, caught in the net, suspended between life and death, their agony rising and falling in waves.

The order spread down the line. The Shadow Paws raised their lacquered claws, and the Tide Wolves beneath them answered. The shadow flare ignited—not the faint, flickering remnant they had carried on the ride, but the full, roaring blaze of the Black Fire's legacy. The wolves' dark fur vanished beneath sheets of flame, and the riders on their backs were engulfed in it, their dark pelts and their lacquered claws and their scarred faces all burning with the same ancient fire that had scarred their ancestors five thousand years ago.

They became a black wave of flame, a hundred burning wolves surging down the ridge toward the purple net.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">On the wall, M'rak saw them. His fur stood up along the back of his neck. His golden eyes went wide. He knew the history. He knew what the shadow flare was, what it had done, what it had cost. And he knew, with a cold, sinking certainty, that if the Plague net and the Black Fire collided, the result would be annihilation. "No—" The word was barely a whisper.</p>
</div>

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">In the sky, Yvaria saw them. She was still aloft on her ghost, her drum harness strapped across her chest, her rune-glass communication shell pressed to her lips. She saw the burning wolves charging toward the purple net, and she screamed into the shell with a voice that cracked with desperation. "NO! NO, FALL BACK! THE PLAGUE WILL—"</p>
</div>

It was too late.

The shadow riders hit the purple net. And something unexpected happened. The wolves passed through. The Plague tendrils did not catch them. The violet light did not trap them. The purple mist did not poison them. The shadow flare—the parasitic fire that had been born in the First Tyrant's madness, the fire that consumed aura and grew stronger with every victim—traveled through the tendrils like lightning through dry grass.

It burned and grew. It ignited the Plague mist, and the mist vanished in gouts of orange and black flame. It climbed the strands of the net, spreading from one orb to the next, consuming the purple light with a hunger that was older than the Plague, older than the Fifth Tyrant, older than the pact itself.

The shadow riders emerged from the other side of the net unscathed. All hundred of them. Their wolves were still burning, their riders were still blazing, and they did not stop. They surged toward the gate, toward the broken wall, toward the city they had ridden through the night to defend.

Behind them, the purple net was coming apart. The tendrils were shriveling. The orbs were flickering. The screams of the trapped were fading into silence. And then a new scream rose from the battlefield—a sound that was not Humman, not Wengari, not any voice that belonged to a living thing. It came from the purple orbs, from the dying Plague net, from somewhere deep beneath the blackened sand. It was a scream of rage, of recognition, of something that had been waiting for this moment for five thousand years and had just been denied.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">The shadow riders reached the gate. Their flames guttered and died, leaving only the faint, flickering remnant that had carried them through the desert night. Zephyr dismounted from his wolf, his scarred face lit by the dying glow of the shadow flare, and looked up at the wall where M'rak was still standing frozen. "We are the Shadow Paws. We were told the White Dawn needed us. We are here."</p>
