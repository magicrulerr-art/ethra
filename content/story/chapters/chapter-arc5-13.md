## Chapter 13: 11:20 — The Shadow Figure Drinks

At 11:20, in the Humman camp, the shadow figure drank from the vial.

The shadowy figure stood alone in the remnants of the command tent, its hood fallen back to reveal a face that was not quite Humman—something older, something colder, something that had been wearing a Humman shape like a borrowed coat. It watched the shadow riders surge through the purple net, watched the black fire consume the Plague tendrils, watched the orbs flicker and writhe and scream. Its nails, long and dark and sharp, tapped against its teeth.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<span class="speech">"Disgusting mongrels."</span> The voice was low and cold and utterly without passion. <span class="speech">"Five thousand years, and they still carry his fire. Five thousand years, and they still ruin everything."</span>
</div>

It reached into the folds of its robe and produced a vial. The glass was dark, almost black, and the substance inside was thick and viscous, a goo that seemed to swallow the light rather than reflect it. The figure uncorked the vial with a single, deliberate motion and raised it toward the distant shape of the Rune-Belt, where the endless darkness dwelled.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"For the Black Dawn."</p>
</div>

It drank. The effect was immediate. The figure's body convulsed, its limbs jerking, its spine arching, its mouth opening in a silent scream that became a gurgle as the black goo began to work. Its flesh dissolved—not melting, not burning, but simply coming apart, losing cohesion, sloughing away in wet, heaping piles that hit the floor of the tent with a sound like raw meat. The heaps began to move. They pulsed and grew, absorbing the fabric of the tent, the wood of the campaign table, the bodies of the dead generals, the blood-soaked sand beneath.

The tent burst outward, its silk walls tearing, its ropes snapping, and what was left of the Humman command post vanished beneath a tide of black, growing flesh.

The Humman soldiers who remained—the survivors of the scorpion vanguard, the Tide Wolf cavalry, the Amuk crews, the servants and the scribes and the merchants who had followed their king to war—watched in horror. The black mass was growing, spreading, consuming everything it touched. It had been their commander, their secret ally, the one who had promised them victory. Now it was a thing of shapeless, pulsing hunger, and it was reaching for them.

They fled. A hundred scorpion riders, their iridescent shells cracked and bleeding. Twenty Tide Wolf riders, their mounts snarling with fear. The last Amuk, its great tusked form trembling, carrying the surviving generals on its back. They fled toward the city, toward the wall, toward the Wengari they had been trying to kill since dawn. The black mass reached for them as they ran. Tendrils of dark flesh lashed out and caught the trailing wolves—three of them, their riders screaming, their bodies dragged backward into the growing heap.

The wolves dissolved, absorbed, and the black mass pulsed with new strength. It began to glow—not the violet of the Plague orbs, but a deeper, older darkness, a light that was not light, a radiance that consumed rather than illuminated.

The remaining Plague orbs—the two that were still intact, still hovering above the battlefield—began to move. They were drawn toward the black mass, pulled by some invisible force, their violet light flickering and dying as they approached. They merged with the mass, and the mass grew larger, darker, more terrible. The black flame that the shadow riders had carried through the net flickered in response, as if recognizing something ancient and hostile.

It was 11:20 in the morning. The shadow riders were at the gate. The wall was broken. The king was still unconscious. And something that had been waiting for this moment since the First Tyrant fell was rising from the ashes of the Humman camp.

Zephyr said, "Open the gates for the humans, don't give it more food"—but no one got to finish the speech. The black thing rose, sixteen meters high, looked at the wall, screamed, and shot green barbs from its mouth. They hit the Woh who fell to the ground writhing, dissolving into green goo pools.</p>
