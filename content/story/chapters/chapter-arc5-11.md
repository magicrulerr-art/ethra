Three more purple orbs rose from the Humman camp. They shot through the morning sky like bruises, trailing tendrils of violet mist, and they did not fly toward the wall. They flew toward the orb that was already there—the one that had been growing above the battlefield since Reva's reinforcements arrived, the one that was now as large as a Woh. The three new orbs stopped in mid-air, each one exactly five hundred spears from the first and from each other, forming a perfect semicircle that arced across the northern approach to the capital.

And then they began to grow.

Tendrils of violet light unwound from each orb, stretching toward the others, linking together like the threads of a spider's web. The first orb reached toward the second, the second toward the third, the third toward the fourth, and the fourth back toward the first, and in the space between them, a curtain of purple light began to form. The tendrils thickened and wove together, layer upon layer, until the entire northern area of the wall was blanketed in a net of violet horror.

The Humman soldiers caught in its path—the remnants of the scorpion riders, the Tide Wolf cavalry, the Amuk artillery crews who had been retreating toward the perimeter—were trapped. They did not die. They did not vaporize. They simply screamed, their bodies suspended in the purple light like insects in amber, their mouths open, their eyes wide, their voices rising and falling in a chorus of agony that was not quite alive and not quite dead.

The sand beneath the net turned black, then purple, then a deep, bruised shade of violet that seemed to pulse with its own heartbeat. The tendrils kept growing, kept weaving, kept spreading, and the net kept thickening, and the screams kept rising. And the shadowy figure in the command tent, standing over the body of the king it had just killed, watched the purple light spread across the battlefield and smiled.
