## Chapter 4: 07:55 — The Dome Shimmers

It was 7:55 in the morning, the seventh day of the Month of Storms, and the golden dome still shimmered over the capital. The Amuk artillery had been pounding against it for nearly an hour, and the light shield had held. But Mekhmed the Third was out of patience.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<span class="speech">"Use the first weapon."</span> His voice was quiet now, the petulant fury of the earlier hours burned away into something colder, something harder. <span class="speech">"I want that shield broken. Now."</span>
</div>

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">The generals exchanged glances. Tamsin, her scarred face set in the cold mask of someone who had been told to shut it and had not forgotten, stepped forward. "Your Wealthiness, that weapon is untested. We don't know if it will work. The Amuks we tested it with—all of them broke their legs. The blowback from the wind sorcery was too much for their frames. We had to sacrifice them. We only have one shot. Perhaps two. If the second Amuk breaks—"</p>
</div>

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"I said use it."</p>
</div>

Tamsin stared at him. The other generals stared at him. The war drums pounded on outside, indifferent to the silence within the tent. Mekhmed's jeweled fingers were white-knuckled on the edge of the campaign table, and his dark eyes were fixed on the golden dome that still shimmered in the morning sun. He had marched nine hundred kilometers to conquer the Wengari capital. He had lost four hundred scorpions in the first twenty-five minutes of the battle.

He had watched the gate nearly break and then watched it be saved by a sorcery no one had told him existed. He was done waiting. Tamsin turned and walked out of the tent without speaking. The other generals followed.

On the battlefield, a Humman artillery crew was preparing to die. They had been told what the first weapon would do to them. They had been told about the Amuks whose legs had shattered, about the crews who had been vaporized by the blowback. They had been told that the weapon was untested, unstable, and almost certain to kill them. They had been told to fire it anyway.

The Amuk beneath them was trembling. It was an old bull, its tusks notched from a dozen campaigns, its eight legs splayed wide to brace against the recoil. The cannon on its back was not like the other artillery tubes. It was larger—a single, massive cylinder of polished steel, its surface etched with wind sorcery runes that glowed a deep, angry red. The Humman crew chief looked at his men. They were young, most of them, their dark eyes wide with the particular terror of soldiers who knew they were about to die.

He raised his hand.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"Fire."</p>
</div>

The cannon erupted. The wind sorcery detonated with a roar that drowned out the war drums, that shook the sand, that sent a shockwave rippling across the battlefield. Every Humman on the Amuk's back was vaporized instantly—their bodies turned to mist by the blowback, their screams swallowed by the thunder. The Amuk's legs shattered. Its great tusked form collapsed into the sand with a scream of agony that echoed across the desert.

The steel ball—a sphere of solid metal infused with compressed wind sorcery—shot through the sky like a meteor. It struck the golden dome. The light shield cracked. A fissure ran across its surface like lightning across glass, a jagged line of brilliant, blinding gold that spread from the point of impact to the farthest edges of the dome. The Wengari inside the city stared up at the sky in horror. The priests in the temple stopped their prayers.

The soldiers on the wall fell silent. The shield held—barely, trembling, the crack sealing itself slowly with a low, resonant hum—but it had been broken. Not destroyed. Not yet. But broken.

<div class="dialogue-block">
Back in the command tent, Mekhmed watched the fissure spread across the dome's surface and smiled. It was not a pleasant smile. <span class="speech">"You see? It worked."</span> He turned to his generals, his jeweled fingers still gripping the edge of the table. <span class="speech">"Again. I want another shot. I want that shield shattered. I want the gate open. Again!"</span>
</div>

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">The generals did not look at him. They were looking at the Amuk that had collapsed in the sand, its legs shattered, its crew vaporized, its great tusked head lolling in the dust. They were looking at the crack in the golden dome, which was still sealing itself slowly, still holding. One shot had broken the shield. Another might shatter it entirely. But another would also kill another crew. Another Amuk. Another piece of the army that Mekhmed had already spent so freely. Tamsin turned to the other generals. "Prepare the second weapon. We have no choice."</p>
</div>

She did not say that they were following the orders of a fool. She did not need to. The war drums pounded on, low and patient and utterly without loyalty. And the golden dome still shimmered over the capital, cracked but unbroken, holding back the storm.
