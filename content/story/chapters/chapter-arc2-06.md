## Chapter 6: Foreign Delegations

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">She straightened, and the faintest smile crossed her weathered face. "I will warn you, though. You have given me cities. You have given me trade. You have given me the first real hope the Pyrinae have had in three thousand years. If you die on this road before I do, I will be very annoyed. Do not die on this road." She turned and walked toward the eastern gate, her rune-glass filaments dimming as she passed into the shadows of the colonnade, leaving the king alone with the setting sun and the spear still quivering in the stone.</p>
</div>


T'van appeared at the arena's edge, his breath short, his shaved mane slick with sweat. He had been running since dawn—carrying messages, summoning delegations, managing the chaos of a capital that had suddenly become the center of the world. His voice, when he found it, was hoarse but steady.


<div class="dialogue-block">
<span class="speech">"The Veylar delegation, my king. A single Shell-Singer. She arrived at the eastern gate this morning and has been waiting in the shade of the outer wall ever since. She says—"</span> He paused, his ears flicking with something between confusion and wonder. <span class="speech">"She says she heard the Styx's cry across the ocean. She says the song of the Deep reached the Tidepools and woke something that has been sleeping for centuries. She says she has come to see the White Dawn with her own eyes before the road begins."</span>
</div>


<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">He hesitated at the threshold. "Ajani... she's beautiful. Not like us. Not like anything I've ever seen. She glows. And the air around her tastes like salt."</p>
</div>


*A Veylar — I've never seen one.*

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"Please send her in, T'van."</p>
</div>


T'van bowed and vanished into the shadows of the eastern gate, his hurried footsteps echoing through the empty arena. For a long moment, there was only silence and the distant cry of the white Styx circling far above.


Then the Shell-Singer entered, and the arena changed.


She was tall, taller than any Wengari, her sinuous body moving with the fluid, hypnotic grace of something that had never known the weight of land. Her iridescent skin shifted as she walked—deep indigo, sea-green, pearl-white—the colors of the Tidepools at dawn. The fin-ridges along her temples and neck rippled faintly, sensing pressure changes, tasting the air. Her eyes were large, dark, layered with a second transparent eyelid that gave her gaze an otherworldly shimmer.


She carried no staff, no weapon, no diplomatic satchel. Only a single Petal-Shell cradled in her webbed hands, its iridescent surface pulsing with a soft, rhythmic light that matched her breathing.


She stopped at the center of the arena, exactly where the other delegations had stood, but she did not kneel. She did not bow. She simply looked up at the throne, her luminous eyes meeting Ajani's across the sand.


<div class="dialogue-block">
<span class="speech">"King Ajani."</span> Her voice was melodic, resonant — a chord struck underwater, the sound of waves retreating from shore. <span class="speech">"I am Sylara, Shell-Singer of the Veylar, Tide-Warden of the eastern reefs, daughter of the moon and the deep water. I have come from the Tidepools to see the White Dawn with my own eyes before the road begins."</span> She raised the Petal-Shell, and its light pulsed once, twice, then steadied into a soft glow. <span class="speech">"I carry a song. The Deep sent it across the ocean. The Styx carried it on their wings. It reached the Tidepools three nights ago and woke something that has been sleeping since the Fourth Tyrant fell. The Abyssal Heart stirs. The Sea Marsh listens. And the Veylar would know what the White Dawn intends, before the world changes again."</span>
</div>


<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"Sylara of the Veylar, Warden of the Tides, this humble king salutes the only race who hasn't been bested by the glory of the Wengari. Please speak, how can this king help the Veylar?" <span class="speech-attr">— 'she does smell like salt... humid, nothing like us'</span></p>
</div>


Sylara inclined her head. Her fin-ridges rippled faintly, tasting the air, reading the currents of heat and pressure that surrounded the throne. When she spoke, her voice was the gentle lap of water against stone, but beneath it lay something deeper—the patient, inexorable pull of the tide.


<div class="dialogue-block">
<span class="speech">"The Veylar do not seek help, King Ajani. We have never sought help. We have endured the Tides since the world was young. We have sung the songs of the deep places while empires rose and fell upon the land. We have watched the Tyrants come and go—the Black Fire, the Fear-on-Wings, the Lightbringer, the Tidebreaker, the Plague. We have survived them all, not because we are strong, but because we are patient."</span> She paused, her luminous eyes meeting his. <span class="speech">"The Shell-Singers heard your coronation. The Styx's cry crossed the ocean. The Lament's song reached the deepest trenches. The Abyssal Heart stirred for the first time since the Tidebreaker fell. You have done something no White Dawn has done since the Lightbringer vanished. You have renewed the pact. You have awakened the ancient ones. And now—"</span> She lifted the Petal-Shell, and its light pulsed in rhythm with her voice. <span class="speech">"Now you intend to build a road."</span>
</div>


<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">The shell's glow intensified, and for a moment, the arena was filled with a sound that was not quite music and not quite language—a resonance that vibrated in the bones, a harmony that spoke of deep water and patient hunger and the slow turning of the moon. "The road will cross the Rune-Belt. The Rune-Belt belongs to the one who dwells there—the one the Tree and the Deep both despise. He has been sleeping since Velarius wounded him. Your road will wake him. The Veylar would know: do you understand what you are about to do? Do you understand what dwells in the heart of the forest? And do you have a plan to face it, when it wakes?"</p>
</div>


<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"I know about the thing that dwells within the Belt, but I do not know how to face him yet. What I do know, with certainty, is that if we build the road before the next Tide, we will weather the Blight. Imagine a corridor that spans the continent... perhaps the Veylar are interested in extending the road to their shores?"</p>
</div>


Sylara was silent for a long moment. The Petal-Shell in her hands pulsed with a slow, rhythmic light, matching the steady beat of her breathing—a sign, perhaps, of deep thought among her kind. The fin-ridges along her temples rippled once, twice, then stilled.


When she spoke, her melodic voice carried a new note beneath its patient cadence. Something that might have been wonder. Or calculation. Or both.


<div class="dialogue-block">
<span class="speech">"The Veylar have never walked the land. We have no roads. We have no caravans. We have no cities beyond the Tidepools. The world above the waves has always been... a curiosity. A place we watched but did not touch."</span> She lifted her luminous eyes to the throne. <span class="speech">"You are offering us a road that stretches from the desert to the sea. A corridor that would connect the Tidepools to the rest of Ethra. Trade. Travel. The exchange of songs and knowledge that has never been possible before."</span>
</div>


<div class="dialogue-block">
She took a step closer, her webbed feet leaving faint impressions in the sand. <span class="speech">"The Veylar do not seek help. We have never sought help. But we are not fools. The Blight stirs. The one in the Rune-Belt wakes. The Convergence approaches. You are building something that has not been attempted since the Lightbringer vanished. If you succeed, the world changes. If you fail—"</span> She paused, her dark eyes unreadable. <span class="speech">"If you fail, we will endure. We always have. But we would prefer to endure in a world that is connected, rather than one that is broken."</span>
</div>


<div class="dialogue-block">
She raised the Petal-Shell, and its light brightened until it cast shimmering reflections across the stone tiers. <span class="speech">"The Veylar are interested, King Ajani. But we do not make decisions quickly. We are a patient people. We deliberate. We sing the songs of the deep places and wait for the moon's counsel. You are asking us to commit to a project that will take decades, perhaps centuries. We cannot give you an answer today. But we can give you this."</span> She extended her free hand, palm up, and a single drop of water coalesced from the dry desert air—a sphere of perfect, shimmering liquid that hovered above her webbed fingers. <span class="speech">"A token. When the road reaches the edge of the Tidepools, send this back to us. We will know what it means. And we will be ready to walk."</span>
</div>


<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">She closed her palm, and the sphere of water drifted across the sand toward the throne, hovering before Ajani like a silent promise. "I have one question, before I go. You said you know what dwells in the Rune-Belt. You said you do not know how to face it. But you are building the road anyway. Why?"</p>
</div>


*Well, I am afraid to learn that you would need to stay, and even if your offer is gracious, we don’t need help either. I am offering a hand in friendship, and you have just quite literally spat on it. The Blight will come for us all. They grew larger since the Fourth Tyrant, and they have never skipped a Tide... I know you know, if we don’t unite, we will dwindle.*


Sylara's fin-ridges went very still. The iridescent shimmer of her skin, which had pulsed with the gentle colors of the tidepools throughout the audience, dimmed to a deep, shadowed blue.


<div class="dialogue-block">
<span class="speech">"You misunderstand,"</span> she said, and her melodic voice carried a new edge—not anger, but something closer to frustration, the controlled patience of an ancient being who had been misinterpreted by a younger race one too many times. <span class="speech">"The Veylar do not spit on friendship. We do not reject your hand. We are a patient people. We deliberate. We sing the songs of the deep places and wait for the moon's counsel. This is who we are. It is not a refusal. It is not a dismissal. It is our way—the way we have endured for ages you cannot imagine."</span>
</div>


<div class="dialogue-block">
She stepped closer to the throne, her webbed feet leaving delicate impressions in the sand, and the Petal-Shell in her hands pulsed brighter. <span class="speech">"But you are right about one thing. The Blight will come. They have grown larger since the Fourth Tyrant. They have never skipped a Tide. And if the surface races do not unite—if the Wengari and the Pyrinae and the Hummans and the Veylar and the Threx and every other thinking being on Ethra do not stand together—the Chi'Thak will devour us all."</span> She paused, her luminous eyes searching Ajani's face. <span class="speech">"You believe the road is the first step toward that unity. You believe that connecting the desert to the marsh, and the marsh to the sea, will bind us together in a way that words and treaties cannot. I do not disagree. But you must understand: the Veylar have been patient for twenty thousand years. We do not rush. We do not leap. We consider. We deliberate. We wait for the moon's counsel. And then, when we act, we act with the full weight of our patience behind us."</span>
</div>


<div class="dialogue-block">
She raised the Petal-Shell, and the sphere of water still hovering before the throne began to pulse in rhythm with its light. <span class="speech">"This token is not a refusal. It is a promise. When your road reaches the edge of the Tidepools, send it back to us. The Veylar will be ready. Not because we were rushed. Not because we were shamed. Because we chose to be. That is how friendship is built among my people. Not with haste. With deliberation. With commitment. With the full weight of the deep water behind it."</span> She bowed her head—not in submission, but in respect. <span class="speech">"The Blight will come. You know this. I know this. The Abyssal Heart knows this. Build your road, King Ajani. The Veylar will walk it when the time is right. Not before. But not after either. At the right moment. The moment we choose."</span>
</div>


*Stubborn little...*

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"Perhaps I am not making myself clear. That is my error. Let me be blunt: by the time the road reaches your shores, your help won’t be needed. A friend doesn’t offer help when it suits them; he does it when it’s needed. You wish to be friend with the Wengari? Then do so like the Wengari do, with the full might behind them. Otherwise retreat to your shores. The road will never reach them, and if it does, it will be your doom."</p>
</div>


Sylara's fin-ridges stiffened. The colors that had rippled across her iridescent skin—deep indigo, sea-green, pearl-white—froze into a single, shocked silver. No one had spoken to a Shell-Singer like this in living memory. Perhaps in any memory.


She stood very still, the Petal-Shell cradled in her webbed hands, the sphere of water still hovering between them like a question neither of them could answer. When she finally spoke, her melodic voice was quieter than before, stripped of its diplomatic cadence, laid bare to something rawer beneath.


<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"You speak of friendship as the Wengari understand it. With claws extended. With fire in your eyes. With the full might of your people behind every promise. This is not wrong. This is who you are. The desert has shaped you, as the tides have shaped us. You are predators. You have always been predators. The strong survive. The weak perish. The pack hunts together or not at all."</p>
</div>


<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">She stepped closer, and the salt air around her intensified, the scent of deep water and ancient patience. "But we are not predators, King Ajani. We are the tide. We are the moon's children. We do not strike. We do not hunt. We endure. We wait. We watch. And when the moment is right—when the water has risen and the stars align and the song of the deep places tells us it is time—we act. Not before. Not after. At the right moment. This is not cowardice. This is not hesitation. This is survival. This is how we have endured for twenty thousand years while the Wengari burned and the Hummans schemed and the Chi'Thak devoured. We are patient. And patience is not weakness."</p>
</div>


<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">She raised the Petal-Shell, and its light flared bright, almost blinding. "You want the Veylar's help? You want our full might behind your road? Then understand this: we have been watching the surface races since the First Tyrant walked. We saw the Black Fire burn. We saw the Lightbringer vanish. We saw the Plague devour. We have watched every Tyrant in history begin with benevolence and end in blood. You are asking us to commit to a White Dawn—to you—before you have proven what you will become. That is not hesitation. That is memory."</p>
</div>


<div class="dialogue-block">
She lowered the shell, and its light dimmed. <span class="speech">"I cannot give you our full might today. The Harmonic Council would never permit it. But I can give you something else. Myself. I will stay. I will walk your road with you. I will see what you build and what you become. And when the time comes—if the time comes—I will tell my people what I have seen. That is not the full might of the Veylar. But it is the full might of Sylara. It is all I have to give."</span> She extended her webbed hand, palm up, empty now, the sphere of water still hovering beside her. <span class="speech">"Is that enough, King Ajani? Or must I retreat to my shores and await the doom you promise?"</span>
</div>


*At last!*

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"While your offer is, I admit, monumental by your standards... what can a single Veylar do? You are not predators, you can’t help us on land. I offer terms: I wish for a contingent of your twenty best craftsmen to help the road. Twenty is not such a large number, I believe. But we are not builders, we are warriors. We can carry the load, but we need someone to shape it. Is this acceptable for the Veylar? Mind you, I do not ask for free labour. They will be paid in full in whatever their currency is."</p>
</div>


Sylara blinked. The iridescent shimmer of her skin, which had flared silver with defiance moments before, rippled through a cascade of colors—surprise, perhaps, or reassessment. The Petal-Shell pulsed softly in her hands.


<div class="dialogue-block">
<span class="speech">"You ask for twenty craftsmen,"</span> she repeated, as if testing the words for hidden meaning. <span class="speech">"Not warriors. Not sorcerers. Not diplomats. Craftsmen. Builders. Shapers of coral and shell and living stone."</span> She was silent for a moment, her fin-ridges fluttering as she processed the request. <span class="speech">"You are correct. We are not builders in the way the Pyrinae are builders. We do not raise monuments. We do not carve cities from the desert stone. But we have shaped the Tidepools for thousands of years. Our coral-work endures the crushing weight of the deepest trenches. Our shell-inlays can channel the Resonant Network across the entire ocean. Our stone-singers can coax living rock into forms that withstand the fury of the Tides."</span>
</div>


<div class="dialogue-block">
She tilted her head, her luminous eyes studying the young king with a new intensity. <span class="speech">"Twenty is not a large number. The Veylar have thousands of craftsmen. But twenty sent to the surface—to the desert, to the Rune-Belt, to places our kind have never walked—that is not a small thing. It is unprecedented. It would require the Harmonic Council's approval. It would require volunteers. It would require..."</span> She paused, and the faintest shimmer of something like humor rippled across her skin. <span class="speech">"It would require payment. You are wise to offer it. The Veylar have no use for Wengari currency. But we have great use for Wengari protection. The Blight will come. The road must be guarded. Twenty Veylar craftsmen, working alongside your warriors, would need to know that their work will not be devoured before it is complete."</span>
</div>


<div class="dialogue-block">
She extended her webbed hand once more, and the sphere of water that had hovered between them pulsed once, twice, then settled into a steady, rhythmic glow that matched the Petal-Shell's light. <span class="speech">"I will take your terms to the Harmonic Council. Twenty craftsmen. Full payment in whatever currency we choose—and I choose protection. Wengari protection for our workers, our worksites, and the completed road. If the Council agrees, the first Veylar craftsmen will arrive at the edge of the Rune-Belt within the year."</span> She met his eyes. <span class="speech">"Is this acceptable, King Ajani?"</span>
</div>


<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"no, I will send Humman envoys guarded by my people to shore, so we can cut the time in half. No objections to that, correct?"</p>
</div>


<div class="dialogue-block">
Sylara's fin-ridges flared, then settled, then flared again in a rapid sequence that might have been the Veylar equivalent of a startled laugh. <span class="speech">"You want to send Humman envoys,"</span> she said slowly, as if making sure she had heard correctly. <span class="speech">"Guarded by Wengari warriors. To the Tidepools. To negotiate with the Harmonic Council in person, rather than waiting for me to carry your terms across the ocean and back."</span> She was silent for a long moment, her iridescent skin cycling through a cascade of colors—silver, deep blue, a flicker of gold that might have been admiration, or might have been alarm. <span class="speech">"King Ajani, the Veylar have not received surface-dwellers on our shores in four thousand years. The last was the Lightbringer. He came alone, himself. He came wandering out of the desert, lost and seeking songs, in the years before he built his pact. He did not ride a Styx—that was Xal'thyra, our shame and our silence, the only Tyrant who ever broke the white flame to her will. The Lightbringer came on foot, with sand still in his mane and the wide eyes of a pilgrim. We gave him our songs. He used them to hold the world together for three thousand years."</span> She raised her eyes to the throne. <span class="speech">"You are not the Lightbringer. You are something new. And you are asking us to open our most sacred places to Hummans—to the race that produced the Plague—guarded by Wengari, the race that produced the Black Fire. All to save time."</span> She paused, and the gold flickered again across her skin. <span class="speech">"The Harmonic Council will debate this for a decade. They will sing the old songs and consult the moon and argue precedent until the Tidepools themselves grow tired of listening. And then—"</span> She inclined her head, a gesture that was almost, but not quite, a bow. <span class="speech">"Then they will accept. Because you are right. The Blight will not wait for deliberation. The road cannot wait for patience. And the Veylar, who have been patient for twenty thousand years, must learn to be swift."</span> She extended her webbed hand once more, and the sphere of water pulsed in time with the Petal-Shell's light. <span class="speech">"Send your envoys. Send your guards. The Tidepools will receive them. I will precede them across the ocean and prepare the Council for what is coming. And when your Hummans arrive on our shores, they will be the first of their kind to see the Coral Citadel since the world was young."</span> She met his eyes, and her melodic voice carried a note that might have been excitement, or might have been terror, or might have been both. <span class="speech">"I have no objections, King Ajani. Only a great deal of fear. And a great deal of hope."</span>
</div>
