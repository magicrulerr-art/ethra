## Chapter 1: The Summons



*What day is it? Who's he?... Ah, the priest! T'van.*

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"Raise your head, T'van. Ajani, please — when you say 'my king' I look behind my back for my father. I'll answer what I can and what I know. Announce me, please, then go to sleep. That is an order."</p>
</div>


T'van's ears perked up at the sound of his own name. The king—Ajani—knew who he was. After everything that had happened, after the whirlwind of the coronation and the endless stream of dignitaries and the bone-deep exhaustion that came from serving a monarch who had just turned the world on its head, the new king remembered the name of a simple priest who had greeted him at the gate.


<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">He raised his head, his golden eyes meeting Ajani's. "Ajani," he repeated, testing the name without the title. It felt strange on his tongue, like a prayer spoken backward. But the king had asked. No, the king had *ordered*.</p>
</div>


<div class="dialogue-block">
<span class="speech">"Yes, my—"</span> He caught himself. <span class="speech">"Yes, Ajani. I will announce you. And then I will sleep. You have my word."</span>
</div>


He rose to his feet, smoothing the front of his robes with paws that still trembled slightly. At the end of the corridor, the great bronze doors of the Hall of the Sun loomed, their surface etched with scenes from the Lightbringer's life: the forging of the pact, the building of the gardens, the vanishing into the Chamber of Reflection. T'van had walked past those doors a thousand times. He had never once opened them.


He drew a deep breath, steadied himself, and pushed.


The Hall of the Sun fell silent as the young priest stepped inside. The elders of the five families—Bright Paw, Stripe Paw, Shadow Paw, Motted Paw, and the representatives of the diaspora—sat in concentric semicircles around a central dais where the Four Pillars stood, still faintly warm from the coronation ritual. The Pyrinae Hydromancers occupied a tiered platform to the left, their rune-glass staves humming softly.

The Humman ambassador, Seris, sat at the edge of the chamber, her scribes flanking her with parchment and ink.


And at the center of it all, an empty throne.


The elders turned. The Hydromancers lifted their heads. Seris raised her pen. And T'van, his voice cracking slightly but growing stronger with every word, spoke the announcement that would begin the council.


<div class="dialogue-block">
<span class="speech">"Elders of the Wengari, honored guests of the Pyrinae and the Humman delegation, I present to you Ajani Brightmane, first of his name, son of Uthgard the Ninth and Layla of the Stripe Paws, Heir of the Lightbringer, Bearer of the Pact, Chosen of the Deep, King of the Wengari, and—"</span> He hesitated for only a heartbeat. <span class="speech">"And the White Dawn."</span>
</div>


The chamber erupted. The Shadow Paws growled. The Stripe Paws thumped their tails. The Bright Paws leaned forward, their golden eyes gleaming with pride and fear in equal measure. The Humman ambassador's pen scratched furiously across her parchment. And the Pyrinae Hydromancers exchanged glances that spoke of ancient knowledge and present dread.


<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">T'van stepped aside, his duty done. As Ajani passed him, the young priest whispered, so quietly that only the king could hear: "I will sleep now, Ajani. And when I wake, I will still be here. Whatever happens." He slipped away through a side door, leaving the king alone before the council that would judge him.</p>
</div>


*Ok... I see everyone is here. Let's get this done so I can sleep too. I should sit on the throne — that seems appropriate. Father always said, if they see your fur bristle, you're as good as dead.*

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"Brothers, esteemed elders, you have summoned me, the king. Tell me, in which way can I serve the Wengari?"</p>
</div>


The throne was cold. It had been empty for a week, ever since Uthgard IX had risen from it for the last time and descended into the Chamber of Reflection to meet his son. No one had sat upon it since. No one had dared.


Ajani settled onto the ancient stone and surveyed the gathered elders. His father's voice echoed in his memory: *If they see your fur bristle, you're as good as dead.*


His fur was not bristling.


<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"Brothers. Esteemed elders. You have summoned me—the king. Tell me in which way I can serve the Wengari."</p>
</div>


The silence that followed was heavy, expectant, charged with the unspoken questions that had been brewing for seven days. It was a Shadow Paw elder who rose first. Old Kareth, his dark pelt streaked with silver, his left eye a milky scar from a battle fought before Ajani's father was born. He leaned on a staff of polished obsidian and regarded the young king with an expression that was neither hostile nor welcoming, but simply... measuring.


<div class="dialogue-block">
<span class="speech">"We have served the Wengari for five thousand years,"</span> Kareth said, his voice rough as sand. <span class="speech">"We served the First Tyrant, who burned half the world and died screaming. We served the Third, who built this city and vanished into the stone. And now, we are asked to serve you."</span> He tapped his staff once against the floor. <span class="speech">"The arena was full of signs, young king. The green fire. The white Styx. The Lament who walked out of the Flickermarch for the first time in three centuries, only to depart without a word the moment your coronation ended. The Pyrinae whisper that the Kyre Tree has not been this active since the Lightbringer's day. The Hummans—"</span> He glanced toward Seris, who did not look up from her parchment. <span class="speech">"The Hummans are terrified. And they have good reason to be."</span>
</div>


He took a step forward, his one good eye fixed on Ajani.


<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"So I will ask you plainly, King Ajani, first of your name, what the other elders are too polite or too afraid to ask. What are you? The White Dawn is a title. Convergence-touched is a description. But what are you? Are you the next Lightbringer—a builder, a uniter, a king who will lead the Wengari to glory? Or are you the next Black Fire? The next Velarius? A Tyrant who will burn everything we have built and leave our cubs to die in the ashes?"</p>
</div>


The chamber fell utterly silent. The Stripe Paws stopped thumping their tails. The Bright Paws held their breath. Even the Pyrinae Hydromancers, who had heard more legends than any living Wengari, leaned forward in their seats. Kareth's scarred eye seemed to pierce through Ajani, through the throne, through the stone itself.


<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"Tell us the truth, young king. Tell us what you are. And then tell us why we should follow you."</p>
</div>


*Kareth. An old general, I believe... The Shadow Paws, blunt as always. At least he is to the point.*

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"Brother, I must be honest — until a week ago I wasn't sure myself of what I was. You know I've been absent for the last years. I travelled, and found myself on the marshes. In there I found a dying Lament — he was burning up with aura, and I saved him. He allowed me to talk with a someone — or perhaps 'something' is the right word. Brother, what I'm about to tell you cannot leave this room, so please have every non-Wengari leave, and only the elders, the chiefs and their heirs remain. I'll wait."</p>
</div>


<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">Kareth inclined his head—a gesture of respect, or at least of acknowledgment. "The king speaks plainly. That is more than I expected. We will wait."</p>
</div>


When the doors closed, the chamber felt suddenly smaller. The concentric semicircles of the Hall of the Sun, designed to hold the representatives of five great families and their honored guests, now contained only the core of Wengari power. The elders of the Bright Paws, resplendent in their golden manes. The Shadow Paw council, their dark pelts blending into the shadows of the chamber's upper tiers. The Stripe Paw chiefs, their massive frames barely contained by their ceremonial armor.

The Motted Paw representatives, sleek and silent, their spotted coats making them nearly invisible against the stone. And scattered among them, the heirs—the sons and daughters who would one day inherit the weight of their families' legacies.


<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">Kareth remained standing. He had not dismissed anyone, but neither had he objected. "The room is yours, King Ajani. Only the elders, the chiefs, and their heirs remain. No words spoken here will leave this chamber without your consent. This I swear on the ashes of my ancestors."</p>
</div>


He tapped his obsidian staff once against the floor, and the other elders murmured their assent. The Stripe Paws thumped their tails. The Bright Paws bowed their heads. The Motted Paws, silent as ever, simply inclined their elegant skulls. The Shadow Paws—the family of the First Tyrant, the family that had carried the shame of the Black Fire for five thousand years—watched with their scarred, unblinking eyes, waiting to hear what the new king would say.


*Ok. Here it goes...*

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"Brothers, my father's last words spoken to me were that I was indeed the White Dawn... He confirmed to me himself I am... the heir of the First, not only in name but in spirit."</p>
</div>


The silence that followed was absolute.


Kareth's one good eye widened. His obsidian staff, which had not wavered in decades of council meetings, trembled in his grip. Around him, the elders of the five families sat frozen—not with the respectful stillness of ritual, but with the raw, electric paralysis of a room full of creatures who had just heard the one thing they had most feared and most hoped to hear. Then Kareth spoke, his rough voice barely more than a whisper.


<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"The heir of the First."</p>
</div>


He did not need to say the name. No Wengari spoke the Black Fire's true name. He was the First Tyrant, the Shadow Paw who had unified the families and burned half the world. And now his heir—not merely a descendant, but an heir in spirit as well as blood—sat on the throne of the Bright Paws.


<div class="dialogue-block">
A Stripe Paw chief rose from her seat. Her name was Zara, and she was the leader of the largest caravan fleet in the Rune-Belt, a warrior who had fought Tide Wolves and Chi'Thak swarms and lived to tell of both. Her massive frame seemed to fill the chamber as she spoke. <span class="speech">"The heir of the First. The heir of the Third. The White Dawn. The Convergence-touched."</span> She shook her great head, her striped mane rippling. <span class="speech">"What does that make you, Ajani Brightmane? You are not merely a Tyrant. You are the heir to every Tyrant the Wengari have ever produced. The Black Fire and the Lightbringer both claim you. You carry their blood. You carry their fire. What else do you carry? Their madness? Their hunger? Their doom?"</span>
</div>


<div class="dialogue-block">
Another voice spoke—soft, almost gentle, but carrying a weight that silenced the chamber. It was the Motted Paw representative, an elder so old that her spotted fur had faded to a pale silver-white. Her name was Nyasha, and she had not spoken in council for three decades. <span class="speech">"The question is not what he carries,"</span> she said, her voice like wind through dry grass. <span class="speech">"The question is what he will do with it. The First Tyrant was a good king for a hundred and fifty years before the madness took him. The Third Tyrant was beloved for a century before he vanished. Tyrants are not born in fire and blood, young Zara. They are made. Slowly. Over time. By the weight of power and the loneliness of the throne and the knowledge that no one else can carry what they carry."</span> She turned her ancient eyes toward Ajani. <span class="speech">"Tell us, young king. You have spoken plainly so far. Speak plainly again. Are you afraid?"</span>
</div>


Kareth stiffened. The Stripe Paws exchanged glances. The Bright Paws leaned forward. And the elders of the Wengari waited for the king's answer.


<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"I would be a fool not to be Elder Nyasha, but what I have to say to you, please I beseech you, do not judge our forefathers too harsh and follow me into the inner chamber... where the Third vanished, you must know what it means to be a Bright Paw, and what the Third did, and what we must do now..."</p>
</div>


Nyasha regarded the young king for a long moment. Her ancient eyes, pale as moonstone, seemed to look not at Ajani but through him—into the chamber below, into the Tree's domain, into the pact that had bound the Bright Paws for three thousand years.


<div class="dialogue-block">
<span class="speech">"You would take us to the place where the Lightbringer vanished,"</span> she said. <span class="speech">"The place where no king has taken anyone since the First built these walls."</span>
</div>


<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">Kareth's scarred face twisted with something between suspicion and unease. "The inner chamber is forbidden to all but the reigning king. It has been so since the Lightbringer's day. Even the elders of the Bright Paws do not enter that place."</p>
</div>


<div class="dialogue-block">
<span class="speech">"Then it is time that changed."</span> Nyasha rose slowly, her spotted fur catching the light. <span class="speech">"The young king offers us truth. Not words. Not promises. Truth. How long has it been since a Bright Paw king offered anything but ritual and tithe?"</span> She turned to the gathered elders. <span class="speech">"I will go. I am old. I have seen two Tides. I will not see a third. If the king is willing to show us what lies beneath, I am willing to follow."</span>
</div>


<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">Zara, the Stripe Paw chief, thumped her tail once against the stone. "And I. The Stripe Paws have carried the Bright Paws' trade for centuries. We have asked no questions. We have accepted the gardens and the wells and the pact. If the king says the truth is buried, then I want to see the shovel."</p>
</div>


One by one, the other chiefs and heirs rose. The Shadow Paws, wary but unwilling to be left behind. The Bright Paws, their pride warring with their fear. The Motted Paws, silent and watchful as always. Only Kareth remained seated, his obsidian staff clutched in both hands, his one good eye fixed on Ajani.


<div class="dialogue-block">
<span class="speech">"I served your father,"</span> he said at last. <span class="speech">"I served his father before him. I have buried two kings of the Bright Paws. I have watched the pact drain them year by year, century by century, and I have said nothing because it was not my place to speak."</span> He rose, slowly, painfully, his old joints cracking. <span class="speech">"If Uthgard's son says the truth lies beneath our feet, then I will see it. But know this, young king. If you lead us into darkness, and there is nothing there but more secrets and more lies, I will not follow you again."</span>
</div>


<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">He met Ajani's eyes, and there was something in his scarred face that might have been hope, or might have been grief, or might have been both. "Lead, King Ajani. Show us what the Lightbringer hid. Show us what you are."</p>
</div>


*I hope the tree doesn't kill anyone. Careful, elders — the steps are steep, the air gets thin. If anyone doesn't feel strong to go on, grab my hand. Just a bit of aura should do. Once we are in the innermost chamber, please do not touch anything, no matter what you see or hear.*


The descent was slow. The spiral stair had been carved by the Lightbringer's own sorcery three thousand years ago, and the steps were worn smooth by centuries of royal footfalls—but only ever one set at a time. No king had brought an entourage into the inner chamber. No king had dared.


Ajani's green fire flickered along the walls, casting dancing shadows that made the ancient runes seem to writhe. Behind him, the elders of the five families followed in single file. Kareth, his obsidian staff tapping against the stone with each labored step, his breath coming in harsh rasps. Zara, the Stripe Paw chief, her massive frame barely fitting through the narrowest portions of the passage, her claws scraping against the walls for balance.

Nyasha, impossibly old, moving with the slow, deliberate grace of one who had walked these depths before—perhaps in another life, perhaps in another age.


The other elders followed. The heirs followed. And at the rear, the Bright Paw priests who had been permitted to accompany their new king exchanged nervous glances as the air grew thin and the weight of the Tree's presence pressed against their lungs.


When Zara stumbled—her great paw slipping on a worn step—Ajani was there. His hand caught her arm, and a pulse of green fire flared between them, steadying her, warming her, filling her lungs with air that did not taste of ancient stone and patient hunger. She looked at him with something that might have been surprise, or might have been gratitude, and nodded once before continuing.


The passage opened. The vast underground chamber lay before them—the roots of the Kyre Tree pulsing with pale green light, the blossom at its center still and silent, the amber pool rippling faintly. The elders, chiefs, and heirs of the Wengari stood at the threshold of the most sacred and terrible place in their history, and for a long, breathless moment, no one spoke.


<div class="dialogue-block">
Then Kareth, his voice rough with awe and exhaustion, whispered: <span class="speech">"The Lightbringer stood here. The First stood here. Every king for three thousand years has stood where we stand now. And we—"</span> He shook his head. <span class="speech">"We are not kings. We are not Tyrants. We are not even priests. Why have you brought us here, Ajani? What must we see?"</span>
</div>


The amber pool rippled. The blossom stirred. The Tree was listening.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"This is the crime of the Lightbringer. This is the truth. What you see here is the lord of the desert. We all live and die under his shadow — the true king, with whom Uthgar brokered peace... with whom every king has paid in blood for our glory. Have you ever wondered why only the strongest among us can live in the Capital?"</p>
</div>


The silence that followed was not the respectful silence of a council chamber. It was the silence of a graveyard. The silence of a room full of people who had just watched the foundations of their world crack open beneath their feet.


<div class="dialogue-block">
Kareth, the old general, the scarred Shadow Paw who had served three kings and buried two of them, was the first to speak. His voice was hoarse, barely a whisper. <span class="speech">"The lord of the desert. The true king."</span> He turned his one good eye toward the pulsing blossom, the roots that twisted through the stone like veins, the amber pool that rippled with ancient hunger. <span class="speech">"We thought the Tree was a myth. A story. The old tales said the Lightbringer made a pact, but we thought—"</span> He shook his head. <span class="speech">"We thought it was a metaphor. A legend. Not... this."</span>
</div>


<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">Zara, the Stripe Paw chief, stepped forward. Her massive frame trembled, not with fear but with fury. "Every king has paid in blood. You said the strongest among us can live in the capital. The weak die. My mother died here, Ajani. She was a Stripe Paw, like me. She came to the capital for a trade negotiation. She fell ill within a week. The physicians said it was a wasting sickness common to outsiders. They said there was nothing they could do. But there was, wasn't there? The Tree was feeding on her. The Tree killed her."</p>
</div>


<div class="dialogue-block">
Her claws extended involuntarily, gouging furrows in the ancient stone. <span class="speech">"How many others? How many Stripe Paws, how many Shadow Paws, how many Motted Paws have come to this city and never left, and we told ourselves it was sickness, it was age, it was the desert's cruelty?"</span> She turned to Ajani, and her eyes were wet. <span class="speech">"How many of us have you fed to this thing?"</span>
</div>


<div class="dialogue-block">
Before Ajani could answer, another voice spoke. It was Nyasha, the ancient Motted Paw, her pale silver fur catching the green light. She had not spoken since the descent began, but now her voice filled the chamber like wind through dry grass. <span class="speech">"The crime of the Lightbringer."</span> She repeated Ajani's words slowly, tasting each one. <span class="speech">"For three thousand years, we have called him the builder, the uniter, the great king. We have named our cubs after him. We have prayed to his memory. And all the while, he was paying a tithe in our blood."</span>
</div>


<div class="dialogue-block">
She turned to Ajani. <span class="speech">"You said you were the heir of the First. But you are the heir of the Third as well. You carry his blood. You carry his pact. You carry his crime."</span> Her ancient eyes held no anger, only a terrible, patient sadness. <span class="speech">"What do you intend to do about it, young king? Will you continue the tithe? Will you feed the Tree as your father fed it, as every king before him has fed it? Or will you—"</span> She paused. <span class="speech">"You said you offered something new. A road. A partnership. The Deep. Tell us. Tell us everything. We have lived in the shadow of this lie for three thousand years. We deserve the truth. All of it."</span>
</div>
