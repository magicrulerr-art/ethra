## Chapter 5: 08:15 — The Second Shot

The second shot came at 8:15.

The temple fell silent before it struck. Every Wengari inside—the merchants and the servants, the wounded and the healers, the old and the young—had heard the first shot break the dome. They had seen the fissure spider across the golden light like lightning across glass. They knew the second shot was coming. They knew there was nothing they could do to stop it. So they closed their eyes. The priests stopped their prayers.

The wounded stopped their moaning. The children stopped their crying. And they waited.

The wind cannon roared. The steel ball struck the dome, and the light shield shattered. The golden hemisphere that had enclosed the capital for an hour fractured into a thousand shards of dying light, each one burning out as it fell toward the city below. For one still moment, the capital was bathed in a soft, golden rain—fragments of ancient sorcery that had been passed from High Speaker to High Speaker since the time of the Third Tyrant, falling like tears into the sand.

Then the light was gone, and the sky was open, and the war drums were pounding, and the scorpions were climbing the wall again.

On the northern wall, Sera raised her lacquered claws. The healed soldiers—those who had been carried to the temple and patched up and sent back to the line—formed up behind her. A new contingent of Pyrinae sorcerers was already chanting, their rune-glass staves glowing with pale blue light, a fresh sheet of ice spreading across the ancient stone. The scorpions' claws found the ice and slipped, and the Bright Paw archers on the parapets loosed a volley into their exposed underbellies, and the first wave of climbers fell back into the sand.

But the Amuks were still firing. The volleys crashed against the wall—fireball after fireball, relentless, indiscriminate, tearing through the Pyrinae chanters and the Bright Paw archers alike. The ice shattered. The scorpions climbed again. The Wengari on the wall fought with claws and spears and the last desperate fury of defenders who knew they could not hold but refused to yield. Sera's elite unit was down to sixteen—four of the wounded had been carried back to the temple, their claws broken, their bodies spent.

The remaining warriors fought on, their dark pelts matted with blood and sand, their Eight Points training turning each engagement into a brief, brutal exchange.

Irek was still on the wall. The young Brightmane soldier who had watched the first wave die with hope in his heart was bleeding from a gash across his shoulder, his spear long since lost, fighting with a broken scorpion claw he had wrenched from a dead rider. He swung it wildly, desperately, and the claw caught a Humman soldier across the chest and threw him from the wall. Irek did not stop to watch him fall. He was already turning to face the next climber.

But the wall was breaking. The Amuk fireballs had torn too many holes in the defenses. The Pyrinae sorcerers were exhausted, their rune-glass staves flickering, their chants faltering. The Bright Paw archers were running out of arrows. The scorpions were cresting the wall in a dozen places, their riders dismounting, their claws finding Wengari flesh and tearing. Sera's elite unit was being pushed back. Step by step. Body by body. The gate, which had held for over three hours, was about to fall.
