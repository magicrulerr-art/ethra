## Chapter 9: 09:00 — The Truce Lasts An Hour

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">He did not need to say who "he" was. The other soldiers within earshot nodded grimly. The truce would last an hour. Perhaps more. The Wengari would use that hour to regroup, to tend their wounded, to wait for reinforcements. The Hummans would use that hour to prepare their next assault, to position their remaining weapons, to pray that their king did not change his mind. The war drums pounded on, low and patient and utterly without loyalty. And the great war was not yet over. It was 9:00 in the morning.</p>
</div>

The purple orb rose from within the Tide Wolf camp like a bruise upon the sky. It was small at first—no larger than a clenched fist—but it pulsed with a sickly, violet light that seemed to drink the morning sun rather than reflect it. Then it screamed. The sound was not a voice. It was not a Wengari voice or a Humman voice or any voice that belonged to a living thing. It was the sound of something that had been trapped for a very long time and had just been released.

The orb shot toward the easternmost section of the wall, trailing a tail of purple mist, and struck the ancient stone with a sound like the cracking of a world. The Wengari manning that section—Bright Paw guards, mostly, the young soldiers who had survived the scorpion assault and the Amuk artillery and the suicide riders—were vaporized instantly. Their bodies did not burn. They did not fall. They simply ceased to exist, their golden manes and their bright eyes and their broken scorpion claws dissolving into the purple mist that now hung over the shattered rampart.

The faint smoke lingered, curling in the morning air like the fingers of a hand that was not quite dead.

On the wall, M'rak and Sera stood frozen. They had been discussing the city's defenses—the remaining Wohs, the elite unit, the Pyrinae sorcerers who were still chanting their ice spells—when the scream had ripped through the air and the purple orb had taken the eastern section. Now the wall was broken, and the mist was still there, and the soldiers who had been standing on that section three heartbeats ago were gone.

M'rak did not speak. Sera did not speak. No one on the wall spoke. They simply stared at the purple mist and the shattered stone and the place where twenty Wengari had been standing.

In the Humman command tent, Mekhmed was laughing. It was not the petulant laughter of the earlier hours, not the cold satisfaction of the suicide scorpions' success. It was something deeper, something older, something that had been waiting for this moment since the first scorpion rider fell at the gate. His generals burst through the tent flaps, their faces pale, their eyes wide with horror.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"WHAT HAVE YOU DONE? WHAT IS THAT?"</p>
</div>

<div class="dialogue-block">
Mekhmed turned to face them. His smile was gone. His face was utterly, terrifyingly serious. <span class="speech">"That is victory. Did you think Velarius started with a big array? No. He made smaller ones. Prototypes. Tests. He built them before he built the Plague, and he left them behind, and I—"</span> He paused, his jeweled fingers tapping against the campaign table. <span class="speech">"I have five of them."</span>
</div>

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">The generals went pale. The young one who had asked about the suicide scorpions spoke, his voice trembling. "Do you even know what you have done? What if they expand? What if they grow, like the ones of the Plague Bearer?"</p>
</div>

As if in answer, the purple orb that had struck the wall began to grow. Tendrils of violet light unwound from its core, stretching across the rampart like the legs of a spider, and twenty more Wengari—soldiers who had been rushing to reinforce the eastern section, who had not been standing in the initial blast but had been close enough to breathe the mist—were caught in its grip. They did not vaporize.

They did not die cleanly. They screamed, and their screams were not the screams of warriors. They were the screams of something that was not quite alive and not quite dead, and the purple light wrapped around them and held them, and the sand below the wall turned a deep, bruised shade of violet.

In the inner chamber beneath the palace, the roots of the Kyre Tree were thrashing. The lord of the desert had felt the First Tyrant's fire five thousand years ago. It had felt the Fifth Tyrant's Plague five hundred years ago. It knew this poison. It remembered the smaller arrays, the prototypes, the tests that Velarius had conducted in secret before he built the great Plague that scarred the world. The roots whipped against the stone walls of the chamber, their pale green light flickering erratically, and the Tree screamed again—a silent, psychic shriek that reverberated through the stone, through the palace, through the bodies of every Wengari who carried its mark.

It was not a scream of pain. It was a scream of recognition. The Plague was not merely a weapon. It was a living thing, and it had just been woken.
