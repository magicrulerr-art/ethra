## Chapter 2: Whitey

**Snow, Ice, and Whitey**

The shout rolled under the ancient arch and into the dark, and the dark gave it back in echoes — and the echoes thinned, and thinned, and went out.

The breathing that had filled the cavern since before any of them were born had stopped. The stirring that had come toward Nikolai's voice had stopped too — very large, and very many, and suddenly no longer excited — and in the steadying pale blue-white glow, the company saw what their Tsar had seen before any of them, and the reason his bellow had carried no challenge in it at all.

Two shapes lay at the far shore of the cavern. Vast, and still, and going dim.

Snow wurms — royal stock, the faint golden hue of Nikolai's own twenty years of breeding still in their chitin, each one longer than three Wohs placed end to end — but the light was leaving them the way heat leaves a forge. Hundreds of legs lay curled and still, the glow fading from segment to segment like a tide that would never come back in. The ice around them was broken — the frozen path gouged and shattered, as if the cavern had been a storm.

Nikolai crossed the cavern in a lurch — the stride of a Tsar coming apart — and fell to his knees between the two fallen.

The Tsar of the Snow Paws wept openly.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"Snow…" One great paw came to rest on the first still head. "…Ice." And on the second.</p>
</div>

The names landed in the cavern behind him.

Vanya had turned away entirely, her muzzle buried in one paw. Nadya's ears had gone flat, and she was examining a crack in the cavern floor as though it were the oldest, most interesting thing under either sun. Anastasia's eyes had closed — only for a heartbeat, only the smallest of flinches — with the expression of one who had been present when these names were chosen.

Cefiro inclined his head, solemn as stone. Beside him, Ajani did the same.

*'Just as my father. Uncle has great naming sense — it must run in the family,'* Ajani thought.

Kira turned to Anastasia. On her shoulder, Pearl's antennae had gone rigid with secondhand shame.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"You named your children, correct?" she whispered.</p>
</div>

Anastasia gave an imperceptible nod.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"All but Cefiro…" she whispered back, regretfully.</p>
</div>

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"Father got her drunk on Frostfire and bribed the priests," Ivan whispered from somewhere behind them, in the hushed tone of one passing down family history.</p>
</div>

The temperature of the cavern dropped away.

Grief, in a Snow Paw, does not end. It changes state.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"WHO?!…"</p>
</div>

Nikolai rose from beside the fallen, and the moisture of the air turned to snow around him, and frost crawled outward across the cavern floor in a widening ring from where he had knelt. He extended one massive paw — and the great halberd materialized from the frozen air, runic patterns blazing along its shaft with ancient power.

His pale blue eyes swept the cavern, and found the two great scorpions.

Blackie and Reddy felt the rage rolling off him in waves. They did not cower — they were apex creatures, and they had led the king down these stairs on urgent business they had not yet told anyone about — but their antennae went high, and they began, with great urgency, to point.

Two tails lifted in perfect unison and aimed at the third wurm. Pincers clicked — rapid, emphatic, unmistakable — the click of two witnesses who had seen the whole thing and were prepared to testify.

*It was that one!!*

The third wurm had pressed itself against the far wall of the cavern, and it was chittering — a frantic, keening, arthropod sound — hundreds of legs scraping in place, its glow flickering pale blue-white like a lantern in wind.

Nikolai approached it, halberd in paw, rage still in the set of his shoulders.

And stopped.

Because the wurm was not pressed against the wall in fear. It was gathered around something.

Its forward segments curved in a cradle, and there, in the hollow of its body, lay a clutch of three massive eggs — pale, faintly golden, each the height of a Wengari's shoulder — and the light inside them pulsed, slow and steady, like heartbeats not yet born.

The halberd lowered, inch by inch, and dissolved back into the frozen air.

<div class="dialogue-block">
<p class="speech-line">"Whitey…" Nikolai said, very quietly. "What have you done…"</p>
</div>
