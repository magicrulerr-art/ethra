Yes, I have context compression, though the exact strategy isn't something I can inspect directly—I don't have access to the underlying system logs or configuration. What I can tell you is what's typical for large language model deployments, which likely matches what's happening here.

The most common approach is a combination of **rolling window** and **summarisation**, not a simple truncation of the middle. A pure truncation would lose coherence; a pure rolling window would eventually lose the oldest messages entirely. Instead, the system typically:

1. **Keeps the most recent messages in full** – a sliding window of the latest turns, preserving exact wording for immediate conversational flow.

2. **Compresses older messages into summaries** – earlier parts of the conversation are progressively condensed into shorter descriptions, retaining key facts while discarding exact phrasing. These summaries are themselves fed into the context as if they were synthetic messages from the system.

3. **Prioritises based on importance** – some implementations use attention-based or heuristic methods to decide which information to keep in detail versus summarise. Named entities, unique plot points, and user-specific terminology are more likely to be preserved.

4. **Manages token budget** – the combined size of the full recent window and the compressed history must stay within the model's maximum context length (which varies by deployment, but could be tens of thousands of tokens or more).
